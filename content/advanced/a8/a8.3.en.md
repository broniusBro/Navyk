::: step hook
It's 3 p.m. on Friday, and Dan has almost used up his session limit. Since the morning he's been working in a single chat: he connected five connectors, asked for every answer at the maximum effort level and ran three in-depth research tasks.

Which of these “ate” the most? And how can he make his limit last on Monday?
:::

::: step explain
According to Claude's help centre, your usage depends on:

- the length of messages and files;
- the length of the current chat;
- tools: {{data.ui_paths.a8_research}}, web search, connectors;
- the model and the effort level {{data.ui_paths.a8_effort_menu}};
- artifacts and multi-step tasks: running code, creating files, working with websites.
:::

::: step explain
The “hungriest” of them:

- **Research** gathers lots of sources, so it uses your limit faster than a regular chat. Available {{data.plans.a8_research_plans}}.
- **Effort**: the higher it is, the more thorough the answer, but it takes longer and uses more tokens. For routine tasks, {{data.ui_paths.a8_effort_routine}} is enough.
- **Connectors and tools** use a lot of tokens: turn them off when you don't need them.
- **A long chat**: Claude takes the whole conversation into account.
:::

::: step summary
**Spend your limit on results, not out of habit.**

Research, high effort, connectors and long chats cost the most. Turn them on for the task at hand, choose a low level for routine work, and check what's left in {{data.ui_paths.settings_usage}}.
:::

Don't include real names, phone numbers or internal company figures — make them up."

  task: "The final practice of the Advanced level. The learner writes one prompt for a big task: an overview of 3–5 tools (CRM, email marketing, accounting, etc.) and a recommendation for their manager. The learner makes up the details. A strong prompt combines the skills of the level: clear goal and who the result is for (R1); context — what the team is, why, budget, requirements, deadline (R2); result format — a document or comparison table, length, structure (R3); criteria for a good result — what matters in the choice, what to avoid (R4); steps in order, e.g. clarify → research → compare → recommend (R5); data and instructions separated with tags or headings, documents above the task (R7); backing from sources — links for prices and features, permission to write “no data”, no guessing (R8). Bonuses, don't mark down if missing: asking for clarifying questions first, an example of the desired table row, mentioning Research or a project for the week-long work. If the learner pasted real personal data or secrets, R10 applies: gently warn them and don't repeat it. This is the celebratory finale of the level: start with what's already strong and acknowledge their progress."
  criteria: ["R1", "R2", "R3", "R4", "R5", "R7", "R8"]

    You're an analyst who helps small teams choose the tools they work with.

    <context>
    I run a flower delivery business in Dublin: 12 staff, 2 of them order managers. Right now we track orders in a shared spreadsheet and we're losing regular customers: we can't remember who sent flowers to whom, or for which occasion. We need a CRM with reminders for repeat orders. Budget: up to €60 a month for the whole team. The director will decide on Friday.
    </context>

    <documents>
    <document name="requirements.docx">[our list of 10 requirements, in order of importance]</document>
    <document name="orders_export.csv">[anonymised export of six months of orders, no names or phone numbers]</document>
    </documents>

    Steps:
    1. If anything is missing for the decision, ask me up to 3 questions first.
    2. Find 4–5 CRMs that are available in Ireland and fit the requirements.
    3. Compare them against the requirements in the document and on price for 12 users.
    4. Give a recommendation: which CRM to choose and why, and what the risks are.

    For every price and feature, give a link to the source. If you can't find a price or aren't sure about a feature, write “no data”, don't guess.

    Result: a 1–2 page document for the director: a comparison table (tool, price, requirements ✓/✗, link), followed by a 5–7 sentence recommendation and 3 risks.

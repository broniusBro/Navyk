::: step hook
Rachel, a marketer in Dublin, has been working in the same chat since morning. In her first message she wrote: “Give all amounts in euros, excluding VAT.” Then she uploaded three reports and spent half the day discussing them. In the evening:

```prompt
Work out the total for the quarter.
```

```claude
Total for the quarter: €85,000 including VAT…
```

Claude “forgot” the rule from the start. Why does this happen?
:::

::: step explain
Let's start with the unit of measurement. A *token* is a small piece of text the model works with: a short word, part of a longer word, or a punctuation mark. Think of them as the bricks every sentence is built from.

Text size for Claude is measured in tokens. For scale: 1M tokens is {{data.limits.a8_million_tokens_words}}.
:::

::: step explain
The *context window* is Claude's working memory within a single chat. Think of it as a desk: everything Claude is working on right now is lying on it.

Everything ends up on the desk: your messages, Claude's replies, files, search results and output from other tools. The desk holds {{data.limits.a8_context_window_paid}}, and other models hold {{data.limits.a8_context_window_other}}. Some of the space is kept free for the reply.
:::

::: exercise multi_choice
id: "a8.1.e1"
type: "multi_choice"
question: "What takes up space in the context window of Rachel's chat? Select all that apply."
options:
  - text: "Her messages, including the first rule about euros"
    correct: true
    why: "Yes, every message is tokens in the window. The rule hasn't gone anywhere; there's just a huge amount of text around it now."
  - text: "Claude's replies"
    correct: true
    why: "People often forget this. Claude's reply at every step becomes part of the conversation and takes up space too. Long replies mean a long window."
  - text: "The three uploaded reports"
    correct: true
    why: "Files are usually the biggest part. Three reports can outweigh the whole conversation."
  - text: "Web search results, if Claude searched the web"
    correct: true
    why: "Yes. Pages it found and output from other tools also land in the window, which is why tools are “heavy”."
  - text: "Everything Claude learned during training"
    correct: false
    why: "No. Training knowledge is the model's general knowledge; it doesn't sit in the window. The window is only what's in this conversation."
hint: "Picture Claude's desk: what's actually lying on it in this chat, and what does it just know anyway?"
:::

::: step explain
So why did Claude “forget”? Anthropic says it plainly: more context isn't automatically better. The more tokens in the window, the lower the accuracy and the harder it is to recall the right thing. This is called *context rot*.

You can pile a thousand papers on a big desk. But finding the one note you need gets harder, even if it's right there.
:::

::: step explain
The takeaway: what matters isn't how much fits in the window, but what's in it.

- New task, new chat.
- Attach only the files you need, not “everything you've got”.
- Repeat an important rule next to your question.
- Long documents at the top, the question at the bottom, as in the lesson on long documents.
:::

::: step summary
**What matters isn't how much fits in the window, but what's in it.**

Tokens are the bricks of text; the window is the working memory of one chat. The more clutter in it, the worse Claude gets at finding what matters. Put in only what's needed and keep your rules next to your question.

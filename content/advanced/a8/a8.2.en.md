::: step hook
Liam has spent nearly four hours planning an office move with Claude: the budget, the floor plan, the removal crew's schedule, emails to the landlord. Suddenly the chat shows {{data.ui_paths.a8_organizing_thoughts}}.

What's that? A glitch? Out of limit? Time to give up?
:::

::: step explain
Nothing to worry about. This is *automatic context compaction*. When a chat approaches the edge of the window, Claude briefly summarises the earlier messages and carries on with the conversation.

The full history is preserved, and Claude can refer back to it. Like lecture notes: the full notebook is still there, but you work from the summary.
:::

::: step explain
Compaction works if {{data.ui_paths.a8_code_execution_toggle}} is turned on in {{data.ui_paths.a8_settings_capabilities}}: {{data.plans.a8_code_execution_default}}.

Two caveats from the help centre:

- long chats like this use more of your limit;
- in rare cases, such as a huge first message, you can still hit the limit.
:::

::: step explain
Compaction is a safety net, not a plan. The summary is shorter than the original, and you don't choose what makes it in.

So keep whatever should always apply somewhere that doesn't depend on chat length: in a *project*. Rules go in the project instructions, reference files go in project knowledge. Claude's help centre recommends projects for long-running work: they load only what's needed into the window.
:::

::: step summary
**Context compaction is a safety net, not a plan.**

The {{data.ui_paths.a8_organizing_thoughts}} message means Claude is summarising the earlier messages of a long chat. The history is preserved, but your limit runs down faster. Keep anything that should always apply in a project: rules in the instructions, files and summaries in knowledge.

::: step hook
Emma keeps her team's tasks in Notion. On Monday she writes to Claude:

```prompt
What's urgent for me this week?
```

```claude
I can't see your tasks or your calendar. Paste your to-do list here and I'll help you prioritise.
```

Copy the tasks over by hand every single week? There's a better way.
:::

::: step explain
A *connector* is a bridge between Claude and a service where your data lives. Think of it as an office key you hand to an assistant: they can let themselves in and take a look.

Once Emma connects Notion, Claude reads her tasks straight from there. No copying, and always the latest version.

Standard connectors from the directory are available on {{data.plans.a4_connectors_plans}}.
:::

::: step explain
The key rule: Claude works *with your permissions*. It sees exactly what you can see in the service, and nothing more.

If Emma can't open the accounts folder, Claude can't either. But if her account can edit tasks, Claude can change them too. We'll come back to this in the lesson on safety.
:::

::: exercise single_choice
id: "a4.1.e1"
type: "single_choice"
question: "Emma connected Notion. The company workspace has pages she doesn't have access to. What will Claude see?"
options:
  - text: "Only the pages Emma can open herself"
    correct: true
    why: "Right. Claude acts on Emma's behalf and inherits her permissions. Whatever is closed to her is closed to Claude."
  - text: "Every page in the company: Claude has special access"
    correct: false
    why: "There's no special access. A connector doesn't get around permissions in the service; it uses your account's permissions."
  - text: "Only the pages Emma created herself"
    correct: false
    why: "The limit is broader than that: what matters isn't who created a page but who can open it. If a colleague shared their page with Emma, Claude will see it too."
  - text: "Nothing, until Emma uploads the pages to the chat as files"
    correct: false
    why: "That's how an ordinary file works. The whole point of a connector is that Claude reads data straight from the service, with no manual uploading."
hint: "Imagine Claude logging in to Notion as Emma. What would it be able to open?"
:::

::: step explain
What a connector gives you:

- **fresh data**: Claude reads the service at the moment you ask, not an old copy;
- **less manual work**: no copying or exporting;
- **actions**: some connectors can write too, for example create an issue in Linear.

What exactly a connector can do is listed on its page in the directory.
:::

::: exercise match_pairs
id: "a4.1.e2"
type: "match_pairs"
question: "Match each task with the most convenient way to give Claude the data it needs."
pairs:
  - left: "Go through the team's new tasks in Asana every morning"
    right: "Asana connector"
    why: "The data changes daily and lives in the service. A connector saves you the daily copying, and Claude sees the current list."
  - left: "Check a contract that arrived by email, once"
    right: "Upload the file to the chat"
    why: "A one-off task with a single document. There's no reason to connect a service for it; just attach the file."
  - left: "Find yesterday's news on exchange rates"
    right: "Web search"
    why: "That's public information on the internet, not your data in a service. You need search here, not a connector."
  - left: "Keep answering questions from the same price list"
    right: "A file in project knowledge"
    why: "The price list rarely changes but is needed in every chat. Project knowledge is the right place for it; you covered this in the module on projects."
hint: "Ask yourself: does the data change often and live in a service? Or is it a single file, public information, or a permanent reference?"
:::

::: step explain
Your turn. Imagine you've connected a task or notes service: Notion, Asana, Linear or something else.

A good prompt for a connector is just like a normal one, plus one thing: say *where* to look. Which service, which database or project, what time period.

Navyk will send your prompt to Claude for feedback.
:::

::: exercise write_prompt
id: "a4.1.e5"
type: "write_prompt"
question: "Write a prompt for Claude with a task service connected: have it put together a plan for the week from your tasks. Don't use real names of clients or colleagues."
placeholder: "Look in Notion, in the “Tasks” database…"
ideas:
  - "Which service, and where exactly are the tasks?"
  - "What time period should it look at?"
  - "What counts as urgent?"
  - "How should the plan be laid out?"
  - "What if there are no tasks or it can't see them?"
min_chars: 60
hint: "Name the service and the place: database, project, board. Add the time period, the rule for picking what matters most and the format you want."
review:
  task: "The learner writes a prompt for Claude with a connector to a task or notes service (Notion, Asana, Linear, etc.). Task: put together a plan for the week from tasks in the service. The lesson teaches that Claude works with the user's permissions and that the prompt should say where to look for the data: service, database/project, time period. A strong prompt also says what counts as a priority, sets a format and asks Claude to say honestly if it can't see the data. The learner may make up the service and details."
  criteria: ["R1", "R2", "R3", "R4", "R8"]
  reference_prompt: |
    Look in the “Marketing team tasks” database in Notion and pick out my tasks due between 13 and 17 October, plus anything overdue.
    I need a plan for the week so I can decide on Monday morning where to start.
    Treat as urgent anything overdue or anything a client needs by Wednesday.
    Format: a table by day (Mon–Fri), no more than three tasks per day, each with its name and due date. Below the table, one line: what can be put off.
    If you can't see some tasks or they have no due date, list them separately instead of guessing.
  show_reference: true
  fallback_feedback:
    - "Have you named the service and the exact place: database, project, board?"
    - "Have you given a time period: this week, dates, overdue?"
    - "Is it clear why you need the plan and what counts as most important?"
    - "Have you set the format: a table, a list by day, length?"
    - "Did you say what to do if tasks aren't visible or data is missing?"
    - "Is the prompt free of real client and colleague names?"
:::

::: step summary
**A connector is a bridge from Claude to your service. Claude sees the same things there as you do, and can do the same things you can.**

In your prompt, say where to look: service, place, time period.

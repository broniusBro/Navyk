::: step hook
Jake spotted a “secret connector that finds discounts for you” on a Discord server. He added it via a link, gave it access to his email and clicked “Allow always” on every one of Claude's questions.

A week later, his email had messages in it that Jake never sent. Where did he go wrong?
:::

::: step explain
Connectors come with three main risks.

1. **Writing.** A connector can not only read but also create, change and delete. Within your permissions.
2. **Unverified server.** Anthropic doesn't verify custom connectors. A malicious server can hide instructions that nudge Claude into doing things it shouldn't.
3. **Third party.** Your data goes to the connector's service, and it has its own rules.
:::

::: exercise match_pairs
id: "a4.5.e1"
type: "match_pairs"
question: "Match each risk with the habit that protects you from it."
pairs:
  - left: "The connector can delete and change data"
    right: "Read approval requests and confirm actions one at a time"
    why: "As long as you confirm every write action yourself, an accidental deletion won't slip through unnoticed."
  - left: "A server from an unknown author"
    right: "Only connect servers from organisations you trust"
    why: "Nobody checks custom connectors for you. Trusting the author is the main filter."
  - left: "A third-party service processes the data"
    right: "Read the connector's privacy policy before connecting"
    why: "Every connector has its own data handling rules. Find out in advance, not afterwards."
  - left: "A connected service you forgot about long ago"
    right: "Disconnect connectors you don't use"
    why: "Unused access is risk with no benefit. Disconnect it and you've closed the door."
hint: "For each risk, find an action you take yourself: before connecting, while working or afterwards."
:::

::: step explain
When Claude wants to use one of a connector's tools, it may ask for your approval. This is your main lever of control.

Only click {{data.ui_paths.a4_allow_always}} for a server and tool you trust to work unsupervised. For anything that writes or deletes, it's better to confirm every time.
:::

::: exercise single_choice
id: "a4.5.e2"
type: "single_choice"
question: "Lucy asked Claude to tidy up her tasks in Asana. Claude asks for approval to delete 40 tasks from the “Archive 2025” project. What's the sensible thing to do?"
options:
  - text: "First ask for a list of the tasks it wants to delete, check it, and only then decide"
    correct: true
    why: "Yes. Deletions are hard to undo. First see exactly what would be deleted, then approve or decline."
  - text: "Click “Allow always” so Claude stops interrupting"
    correct: false
    why: "That gives Claude the right to delete without asking next time too. That's far too much trust for deleting."
  - text: "Approve it: if Claude suggested it, it must be right"
    correct: false
    why: "Claude may have misunderstood “tidy up”. Approval is your check, not a formality."
  - text: "Disconnect the Asana connector for good, right away"
    correct: false
    why: "Too drastic. Claude did the right thing by asking for permission. Just check the list and decide."
hint: "Think about whether this action is easy to undo and what you know about those 40 tasks right now."
:::

::: step explain
One last technique: agree on the rules with Claude up front. With a new connector, ask it to:

- first tell you what tools the connector has;
- flag which of them change or delete data;
- do nothing irreversible without your “yes”.

Navyk will send your prompt to Claude for feedback.
:::

::: step summary
**A connector acts on your behalf. Only connect what you need and trust, read approval requests and disconnect what you don't use.**

{{data.ui_paths.a4_allow_always}} is only for what you trust to work unsupervised.

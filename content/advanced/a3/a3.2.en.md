::: step hook
Emma from Manchester has three tasks today:

1. Find out what time the repair centre opens tomorrow.
2. Using her own numbers, work out which is better: overpaying her mortgage or putting the money into savings.
3. Compare 6 online booking apps for her nail salon.

Same tool for all three? Let's work it out.
:::

::: step explain
Claude has three tools for tasks like these:

- **Web search**: a quick, fresh fact. 1–2 searches and the answer's ready.
- **Thinking**: hard reasoning without fresh data. Calculations, logic, analysing a document, code.
- **Research**: a big gathering job across many sources. {{data.limits.a3_research_tool_calls}} searches and a report with links.
:::

::: exercise match_pairs
id: "a3.2.e1"
type: "match_pairs"
question: "Match Emma's tasks with the right tool."
pairs:
  - left: "What time the repair centre opens tomorrow"
    right: "Web search"
    why: "One fresh fact from a website. Search will find it in a couple of queries; a big investigation would be overkill."
  - left: "Overpay the mortgage or save the money — based on her numbers"
    right: "Thinking"
    why: "Emma already has all the data; what she needs is a careful calculation and a comparison of options. The internet isn't the main thing here."
  - left: "Compare 6 online booking apps on price and features"
    right: "Research"
    why: "Six products, each with its own website and reviews. That's lots of searches in a row plus a summary report — a job for Research."
hint: "For each task, ask: does it need a fresh fact, long reasoning or lots of sources?"
:::

::: step explain
Where to turn on thinking: click {{data.ui_paths.a3_model_name}}, hover over {{data.ui_paths.a3_effort}} and choose a level. The {{data.ui_paths.a3_thinking}} toggle is there too.

Some models always think; then you only control the effort level. The higher it is, the longer the answer takes and the faster you use up your limit.
:::

  - text: "Raise the effort level or turn on thinking"
    correct: true
    why: "Everything needed is already in the agreement. The job is to carefully connect the clauses to each other, and that's where thinking helps."
  - text: "Research"
    correct: false
    why: "Research gathers information from many sources online. Here there's one source — the agreement — and Claude already has it."
  - text: "Web search"
    correct: false
    why: "Search would bring up general articles about renting, but Jack needs an analysis of his specific agreement."
  - text: "Nothing; the answer to a question like this is always short"
    correct: false
    why: "Analysing a long document is exactly the kind of task where more effort helps. The default settings are fine for simple questions."
hint: "Where does the data for the answer come from: the internet, or what's already in the chat?"
:::

::: step explain
You can combine the tools. Thinking helps plan the work, and Research gathers the material.

For example: “prepare an overview of new technologies for a business proposal” or “compare the findings of several scientific papers”. You need both broad searching and a thoughtful conclusion.

Research is available {{data.plans.a3_research_plans}}.
:::

::: step summary
**Fact: search. Reasoning: thinking. Many sources: Research.**

For simple tasks, the default settings are enough. Turn on the extras when the task is worth it: they use up your limit.

Next: running Research for real.
:::

::: step hook
Olivia in London wants to open a coffee shop near a Tube station, and she asked Claude, with search on, about nearby competitors. She got three links and a couple of generic sentences.

But she needs the full picture: who's nearby, what they charge, what reviews praise and complain about. Regular search isn't enough. She needs *Research*.
:::

::: step explain
*Research* is a mode where Claude runs the investigation itself. It does a series of searches, each one building on what it's found so far, and decides on its own what to look into next.

Like an intern given a day to study the market: they won't stop at the first page of results. In {{data.limits.a3_research_time}}, you get a report with links.
:::

::: step explain
To start it: tap {{data.ui_paths.chat_plus_menu}}, choose {{data.ui_paths.a3_research}}, then ask your question.

- Research is available {{data.plans.a3_research_plans}}.
- Web search needs to be on.
- If the research doesn't start, say it directly: “Use Research to…”.
- If Gmail, Google Calendar or Google Docs are connected, Claude searches there too.
:::

::: step explain
Research uses up your limit faster than regular chat: lots of searches and a long report. So it's important to say from the start what “done” means.

*Success criteria* are the signs of a good report: how many options to compare, on which parameters, and how to present them. Without them, Claude decides on its own where to stop.
:::

::: exercise find_error
id: "a3.3.e3"
type: "find_error"
question: "Olivia launches Research. Tap the fragments that are missing success criteria or a way to check the results."
prompt: "Look into coffee shops near Clapham North Tube station in London: compare at least 5 on cappuccino price, opening hours and what reviews praise and complain about. Just do whatever works. Sources don't matter, the conclusion is what counts."
segments:
  - text: "Look into coffee shops near Clapham North Tube station in London: "
    weak: false
    why: "It's clear what to research and where. Good boundaries for the investigation."
  - text: "compare at least 5 on cappuccino price, opening hours and what reviews praise and complain about. "
    weak: false
    why: "These are the success criteria: how many options and on which parameters. Claude knows when the job is done."
  - text: "Just do whatever works. "
    weak: true
    why: "An empty phrase instead of a format. Better: “a table of the coffee shops, with 3 takeaways for my new coffee shop below it”."
  - text: "Sources don't matter, the conclusion is what counts."
    weak: true
    why: "Without sources, the conclusion can't be checked, and Olivia is going to build a business on it. Better: “a link for every figure; if the data disagrees, show it”."
hint: "Find the spot where the shape of the result isn't set, and the spot that makes it impossible to check the facts."
:::

::: step explain
The report is in. Don't rush to forward it to your business partner.

Check the three most important facts: open the link and find what the report says. If there's no link or the source says something different, ask Claude to recheck that specific point.
:::

::: exercise multi_choice
id: "a3.3.e4"
type: "multi_choice"
question: "The Research report says: “The average cappuccino price at competitors is £3.40.” How do you check this conclusion? Select all sensible steps."
options:
  - text: "Open the links next to the prices and make sure the figures really are there"
    correct: true
    why: "This is the core citation check: the source should say exactly what the report says."
  - text: "Check how old the menus on the websites are"
    correct: true
    why: "Prices change. A two-year-old menu will give the wrong average."
  - text: "Ask which coffee shops the average was based on"
    correct: true
    why: "That shows what the conclusion rests on: how many coffee shops were included and whether any from another area slipped in."
  - text: "Trust it: it's Research, so it has already checked everything"
    correct: false
    why: "Research searches more thoroughly, but a human still checks the citations. Especially the figures a decision rests on."
hint: "Check three things: does the figure match the source, is the source recent, and what went into the average."
:::

::: exercise write_prompt
id: "a3.3.e5"
type: "write_prompt"
question: "Write a Research prompt for a real task of your own: choosing a service, comparing suppliers, courses, a neighbourhood to move to. Add success criteria and requirements for sources."
placeholder: "Look into and compare…"
ideas:
  - "What to compare and how many options"
  - "On which parameters"
  - "Why you need it: what decision you'll make"
  - "Report format: table, takeaways"
  - "A link for every fact, show disagreements"
min_chars: 80
hint: "Describe what to research and where, how many options to compare and on which parameters, and what decision you'll make. Ask for a table, a link for every fact and an honest note when data is missing."
review:
  task: "Lesson on Research mode (Claude runs a series of searches itself and writes a report with links). The learner writes a Research prompt for their own task. A strong prompt: it's clear what to research and where (R1); it explains what decision the learner will make and why (R2); the report format is set — table, takeaways, length (R3); there are success criteria — how many options, which parameters, when the job is done (R4); it relies on sources — a link for every fact, disagreements shown, “not found” is acceptable (R8). Don't require the prompt to mention Research mode: the learner turns it on in the interface. If the prompt contains other people's personal data or passwords, R10 applies."
  criteria: ["R1", "R2", "R3", "R4", "R8"]
  reference_prompt: |
    Look into online booking systems for a small beauty salon in the UK (2 stylists). Compare at least 5 on monthly price, free plan, client reminders by SMS or WhatsApp, and taking deposits when clients book.
    I need to pick one by the end of the month and move all our bookings there; right now everything's in a notebook and Instagram DMs.
    Report: a table of the services, with 3 takeaways below it — which one fits best and why.
    For every price and feature, give a link to the source; prefer official websites. If the data differs between sites or you can't find something, flag it — don't guess.
  show_reference: true
  fallback_feedback:
    - "Subject: is it clear what to research and where?"
    - "Criteria: did you say how many options to compare and on which parameters?"
    - "Why: did you explain what decision you'll make?"
    - "Format: did you set the shape of the report — table, takeaways?"
    - "Sources: are you asking for a link for every fact and a note on disagreements?"
:::

::: step summary
**Research is for tasks with many sources where you need an overall conclusion.**

Before you start, say what “done” means: how many options, which parameters, what format. Afterwards, check the citations for the most important facts.

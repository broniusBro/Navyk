::: step hook
Andrew from Leeds is heading to Warsaw for the weekend and asks:

```prompt
How much is a public transport ticket in Warsaw right now?
```

```claude
As far as I know, a single ticket costs about 4 złoty, but prices may have changed…
```

“As far as I know” and “may have changed”: Claude is honestly admitting it doesn't know current prices. So how do you get an up-to-date answer?
:::

::: step explain
Without search, Claude answers from what it knew when it was trained. Current prices, news and timetables aren't in there.

*Web search* means Claude looks things up online itself and answers with links to its sources. Like a tour guide who doesn't rely on memory but opens an up-to-date guidebook and shows you the page.
:::

::: step explain
To turn it on: tap {{data.ui_paths.chat_plus_menu}} and choose {{data.ui_paths.a3_web_search}}. Tap it again to turn search off.

Search is available {{data.plans.a3_web_search_plans}}.

In the new interface there may be no toggle at all: Claude searches on its own when it helps. To make sure it searches, start with “Search the web for…”.
:::

::: exercise single_choice
id: "a3.1.e1"
type: "single_choice"
question: "Which question should you definitely ask with web search on?"
options:
  - text: "“Is the POLIN Museum in Warsaw open tomorrow on the public holiday, and until what time?”"
    correct: true
    why: "Holiday opening hours are a fresh, specific fact. It isn't in Claude's knowledge; you need the museum's official website."
  - text: "“Explain in simple terms how a debit card is different from a credit card”"
    correct: false
    why: "This is settled knowledge; it doesn't change every week. Claude can explain it without search, and you'll save your limit."
  - text: "“Rewrite my email to my boss so it sounds more polite”"
    correct: false
    why: "This is work on your own text. You don't need the internet: everything that matters is already in the email."
  - text: "“Come up with 5 names for a coffee shop”"
    correct: false
    why: "A creative task. Search adds nothing; it just costs time and limit."
hint: "Ask yourself: could the answer have changed in the last month? Do you need a specific website?"
:::

::: step explain
An answer with search comes with *citations*: links to sources sit next to the claims. But a link isn't a check yet.

Open the source for the main fact and ask yourself:

- does it say exactly this?
- is the page recent?
- who wrote it: an official site or someone on a forum?

For important decisions, compare at least two sources.
:::

::: exercise multi_choice
id: "a3.1.e2"
type: "multi_choice"
question: "Claude used search to tell you which documents you need for a residence permit application, and gave 4 links. What's sensible to do before you go to the immigration office? Select all that apply."
options:
  - text: "Open the link next to the most important point and find that statement there"
    correct: true
    why: "A link next to the text doesn't guarantee the source says exactly that. Find the right spot yourself."
  - text: "Check that at least one source is the agency's official website"
    correct: true
    why: "For documents, the primary source is the agency itself. A blog or forum may be repeating old rules."
  - text: "Check when the pages were last updated"
    correct: true
    why: "Rules change. A three-year-old article may describe a process that no longer applies."
  - text: "Trust the whole answer, since it has links"
    correct: false
    why: "Links are a reason to check, not a guarantee. The source may say something else, and some links don't open at all."
  - text: "Ask Claude to repeat the answer more confidently"
    correct: false
    why: "A confident tone doesn't make a fact any truer. You check the source, not the tone of voice."
hint: "Think about three things: does the text match the source, who wrote it, and how recent is the page."
:::

::: exercise find_error

segments:
  - text: "Search the web for which vaccinations a dog needs to travel to France. "
    weak: false
    why: "A good start: it's clear what to look for, and “search the web” triggers a search."
  - text: "Don't give me links, just list them. "
    weak: true
    why: "Without links, Natalie can't check a single point. With entry rules for pets, that's risky: one mistake and the dog could be turned away at the border."
  - text: "If websites say different things, pick any option."
    weak: true
    why: "A disagreement is an important signal; it shouldn't be hidden. Better: “if sources disagree, show both versions and say which one is official”."
hint: "Look for fragments that take away your ability to check the answer or hide contradictions."
:::

::: exercise match_pairs
id: "a3.1.e4"
type: "match_pairs"
question: "You're checking an answer that used search. Match each situation with the right action."
pairs:
  - left: "The link doesn't open"
    right: "Ask for another source for that fact"
    why: "Some links really can be broken. A fact without a working source isn't confirmed yet."
  - left: "The source is an article from 2021"
    right: "Ask for a more recent page"
    why: "Prices, rules and timetables change. An old page only works for facts that don't change."
  - left: "A claim has no link"
    right: "Ask to back it up with a source or mark it as unverified"
    why: "Without a link, you can't tell whether Claude found it or made it up. Ask for a source or an honest label."
  - left: "The source is a forum thread"
    right: "Ask to find the official website"
    why: "A forum retells other people's experience. For rules and prices, the primary source is the organization that sets them."
hint: "For each situation, find the action that brings you back to a reliable, recent source."
:::

::: step explain
Search uses up your limit, and reading long pages even more so. Turn it on when you need a fresh fact, and off for everyday tasks.

A strong search prompt says what to find, why, which sources to prefer and what to do when they disagree. Try it yourself.
:::

::: exercise improve_prompt
id: "a3.1.e5"
type: "improve_prompt"
question: "Improve the prompt so Claude with search gives you an answer you can verify: what exactly to find, why, which sources, and what to do when they disagree. Use a real task of your own or keep the example."
weak_prompt: "Find what documents I need for a passport"
placeholder: "Search the web for…"
ideas:
  - "Who it's for and in which country"
  - "Why: applying yourself, for a child, urgently?"
  - "Which sources to prefer: official websites"
  - "A link for every point"
  - "What to do if sources disagree"
min_chars: 60
hint: "Add three things: specifics (who, where, when), a requirement for sources (official, with a link for every point) and a rule for when sources disagree or nothing turns up."
review:
  task: "Lesson on web search: the learner improves a prompt that Claude will run with web search. A strong prompt: it's clear what to find, for whom and where (R1); there's context — why, the situation, the deadline (R2); the shape of the answer is set — list, steps, table (R3); it's clear what a good answer looks like — recent, to the point (R4); it relies on sources — a link for every point, official sources first, disagreements shown, “not found” is acceptable (R8). The learner may use their own task instead of the passport example — that's a plus. If the prompt contains document numbers or other people's personal data, R10 applies."
  criteria: ["R1", "R2", "R3", "R4", "R8"]
  reference_prompt: |
    Search the web for the documents I need to get a first UK passport for my 10-year-old. Their mum and I will apply together in a month, and I want to have everything ready so we only have to do it once.
    Rely on official government websites; use other sites only as a supplement and flag it when you do.
    Answer: a numbered list of documents, with a link to the source for each point and the page's last-updated date if it's shown. Separately: processing time and cost.
    If sources disagree, show both versions and say which one is official. If you can't find something, say so — don't guess.
  show_reference: true
  fallback_feedback:
    - "Specifics: is it clear what to find, for whom and where?"
    - "Why: did you explain the situation or deadline?"
    - "Format: did you set the shape of the answer — list, table, steps?"
    - "Sources: are you asking for a link for every point, with official sites first?"
    - "Disagreements: did you say what to do if sources differ or nothing turns up?"
    - "Privacy: is the prompt free of document numbers and other people's personal data?"
:::

::: step summary
**Need a fresh fact? Turn on search. A link is a reason to check, not a guarantee.**

Open the source for the main fact, look at the date and the author, and for anything important, compare two sources.

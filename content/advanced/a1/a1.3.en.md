::: step hook
Mark from Waterford has a report for management on Friday: how the quarter went in the delivery department. He has notes on his phone, a spreadsheet of figures and 40 minutes.

In the past he'd have spent half the night pushing boxes around in PowerPoint. Today he's trying **Claude Slides**.
:::

::: step explain
Slides builds a presentation from what you already have: notes, a report, a document or even the conversation in this chat.

How to start:

- ask in the chat: “Make a presentation…” or choose {{data.ui_paths.a1_output_slides}};
- pick a template in {{data.ui_paths.a1_artifacts_tab}};
- or ask Claude to turn a finished document from Docs into a presentation.

Slides is available {{data.plans.a1_templates_plans}}.
:::

  - text: "Start a new chat and retell the report from memory"
    correct: false
    why: "Retelling from memory loses figures and details. Better to build on the finished document."
  - text: "He can't: a presentation can only be made from scratch"
    correct: false
    why: "Building presentations from notes, reports and documents is exactly what Slides does."
hint: "Think about where all the material already lives and whether it can be used directly."
:::

::: step explain
A good prompt answers a presentation designer's questions:

- **Who and why:** who's listening, and what should they decide or remember?
- **How much:** minutes of speaking time or number of slides.
- **From what:** your notes and figures, not generic statements.
- **How:** one takeaway per slide, little text, charts where needed.

Without this you'll get a neat but average presentation “about everything”.
:::

::: exercise find_error
id: "a1.3.e2"
type: "find_error"
question: "Mark wrote a prompt. Tap the fragments that will make the presentation end up “about everything”."
prompt: "Make a presentation from my notes below for my report to management on Friday. Add lots of slides so it looks impressive. Throw in some numbers about the delivery market."
segments:
  - text: "Make a presentation from my notes below "
    weak: false
    why: "Good: Claude will work from real notes, not general knowledge."
  - text: "for my report to management on Friday. "
    weak: false
    why: "The audience and the occasion are clear. He could add what management needs to decide, but the basics are there."
  - text: "Add lots of slides so it looks impressive. "
    weak: true
    why: "More slides don't make a report impressive. Better to set limits: “8–10 slides for 10 minutes, one key takeaway on each”."
  - text: "Throw in some numbers about the delivery market."
    weak: true
    why: "“Some numbers” is an open invitation to make things up. If figures are needed, provide them yourself, or ask Claude not to invent any and to flag where data is missing."
hint: "Look for places where Mark asks for “lots of” and “some” instead of specifics."
:::

::: exercise build_prompt
id: "a1.3.e3"
type: "build_prompt"
question: "Build a strong prompt for Mark's presentation. Leave out any blocks that don't belong."
blocks:
  - id: "b1"
    text: "Make a Slides presentation from my notes below: Q3 results for the delivery department."
    role: "goal"
    why: "It's clear what to make and from what: a presentation based on real notes."
  - id: "b2"
    text: "I'm presenting to management on Friday, 10 minutes. The main goal is to get them to approve two more couriers for December."
    role: "context"
    why: "Audience, timing and the purpose of the talk. Claude will structure the slides around the decision Mark needs."
  - id: "b3"
    text: "8–10 slides, each with one key takeaway in the title and no more than three bullet points. Show the figures from the spreadsheet as charts."
    role: "format"
    why: "The format is given as “what to do”: how many slides, what goes in the title, where the charts go."
  - id: "b4"
    text: "Only use figures from my notes. If something is missing for a conclusion, add a “to check” note on the slide."
    role: "format"
    why: "Protection against made-up details: figures only from Mark's data, gaps flagged rather than filled in."
  - id: "b5"
    text: "Make it look stylish and modern, like Apple."
    role: "noise"
    why: "It sounds clear, but says nothing about the content. And a management report doesn't need another brand's style."
  - id: "b6"
    text: "Feel free to add a few interesting facts about deliveries around the world."
    role: "noise"
    why: "It drifts away from the goal and invites unchecked figures. Management needs the department's results, not trivia."
target_order: ["b1", "b2", "b3", "b4"]
hint: "The order: what to make, who it's for and why, how to format it, what not to make up. Leave out the nice-sounding but empty wishes."
:::

::: step explain
The presentation is ready. What next:

- **Edit any slide right in Claude:** text, order, design. Or ask: “Split slide 4 into two.”
- **Present without leaving Claude:** move through slides with the space bar or the right arrow.
- **Export** via {{data.ui_paths.a1_export}}: {{data.limits.a1_slides_export_formats}}.
- **Share** via {{data.ui_paths.a1_share}}: you can give colleagues view, comment or edit access.
:::

::: step summary
**Claude Slides turns notes, documents and conversations into a presentation you can edit, present and export.**

A strong prompt covers who it's for and why, how many slides, what material, how to lay it out, and no “some numbers”.

::: step hook
David runs the sales team at a distribution company in Antwerp. Their CRM is connected to Claude. Every Monday the director asks: “So, how are the deals looking?”

David used to spend an hour exporting spreadsheets. Now he wants Claude to put together a dashboard in a couple of minutes. The key is to ask the right question and check what's been counted.
:::

::: step explain
**Claude Dashboards** build charts from your data. The feature is in beta and available on {{data.plans.a1_dashboards_plans}}.

Sources: {{data.limits.a1_dashboards_sources}}.

How to start: ask in the chat, choose {{data.ui_paths.a1_output_dashboards}} or pick a template in {{data.ui_paths.a1_artifacts_tab}}.

Claude writes a *data query* (SQL) for each chart itself.
:::

::: exercise single_choice
id: "a1.7.e1"
type: "single_choice"
question: "Which of David's prompts is best suited to a dashboard?"
options:
  - text: "“Which deals in Salesforce have been stuck at the same stage for more than 30 days, and which reps have the most of them?”"
    correct: true
    why: "It's a question about what David wants to know, and it names the app. That's exactly what the help centre recommends: ask a question, don't name a chart."
  - text: "“Make a pie chart”"
    correct: false
    why: "There's no telling what to show or where to get the data. A chart type without a question doesn't help."
  - text: "“Make a nice sales dashboard”"
    correct: false
    why: "“Sales” is too broad: revenue, deals, reps, regions? Claude will have to guess what matters to David."
  - text: "“What's the average revenue of distributors in Belgium?”"
    correct: false
    why: "That's a question about the market, not the company's data. A dashboard is built from connected sources, not from general knowledge."
hint: "The help centre recommends asking about what you want to know and naming the table or app."
:::

::: exercise find_error
id: "a1.7.e2"
type: "find_error"
question: "David wrote a prompt. Tap the fragments where Claude will have to guess."
prompt: "Using our Salesforce data, show how the number of closed deals changed week by week since the start of the quarter. Add anything the director might find interesting. Don't count the failed deals, you know which ones."
segments:
  - text: "Using our Salesforce data, "
    weak: false
    why: "The source is named. Claude won't have to guess which system to look in."
  - text: "show how the number of closed deals changed week by week since the start of the quarter. "
    weak: false
    why: "A clear question with a period and a step: weekly, since the start of the quarter."
  - text: "Add anything the director might find interesting. "
    weak: true
    why: "“Anything interesting”: Claude doesn't know what matters to the director. Better to name it: “and revenue by sales rep”."
  - text: "Don't count the failed deals, you know which ones."
    weak: true
    why: "In a CRM, “failed” could mean lost, cancelled or simply abandoned deals. Name the status: for example, “with the status Closed Lost”."
hint: "Look for phrases that would make a colleague ask: “Meaning what exactly?”"
:::

::: step explain
Every chart shows **its data query** and **when it was last refreshed**. That's your check.

Open the query and look: are these the right deals, the right period, the right status? If it's counting the wrong thing, tell Claude directly: “Only count deals with the status Closed Won.”

Dashboards are built for quick questions. For deeper analysis, the help centre suggests moving the data into another tool.
:::

options:
  - text: "Open that chart's query and see which deals it's counting"
    correct: true
    why: "The query shows how the count works. Maybe it included all deals, not just the won ones."
  - text: "Check when the data was last refreshed"
    correct: true
    why: "Every chart shows its refresh time. Stale or partly loaded data can produce strange numbers too."
  - text: "Ask Claude: “Only count deals with the status Closed Won and show me what changed”"
    correct: true
    why: "A specific edit in the chat. Claude will rewrite the query, and you'll see whether the anomaly goes away."
  - text: "Send it to the director straight away: “We've hit a record!”"
    correct: false
    why: "A 3.5× jump is a reason to check, not to celebrate. A mistake in a report to the director costs more than five minutes of checking."
hint: "A strange number is a signal to check what was counted and when."
:::

::: step summary
**A dashboard starts with a question, not a chart: ask what you want to know and name the source.**

Every chart has a data query and a refresh time. A strange number is a reason to open the query and clarify what to count, not to take it to the director.

You've finished the Artifacts module! Next up: documents and spreadsheets.
:::

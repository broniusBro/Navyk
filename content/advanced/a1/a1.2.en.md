::: step hook
Sarah from Galway needs the rules for a drawing competition at her library. In the past she'd have asked Claude in the chat, copied the text into Word, edited it there, then gone back to ask Claude to rewrite a paragraph.

Now the document lives in one place: Claude writes, Sarah edits, a colleague leaves comments, and at the end it goes to Word with one click.

That's **Claude Docs**.
:::

::: step explain
How to start a document:

- just ask in any chat: “Make a document…”;
- start your message with {{data.ui_paths.a1_docs_command}};
- or choose {{data.ui_paths.a1_output_docs}}.

You can also start from a template in {{data.ui_paths.a1_artifacts_tab}}. Docs is available {{data.plans.a1_templates_plans}}.

Claude will first ask a couple of questions, then write a draft with comments on its choices. Answer them up front, right in your prompt: **what the document is for, who's reading it and what goes in it.**
:::

  - text: "Make a document: rules for the “My Town” children's drawing competition at our local library. "
    weak: false
    why: "It's clear what the document is and what it's about. A good start."
  - text: "Parents will be reading it, "
    weak: false
    why: "It's clear who the document is for. Claude will write in plain language, without library jargon."
  - text: "format it the usual way. "
    weak: true
    why: "Claude doesn't know what “the usual way” is. Better to list the sections: age groups, entry format, deadlines, prizes, contacts."
  - text: "Add the dates, you know which ones."
    weak: true
    why: "Only Sarah knows the dates. Claude will make them up, and parents will believe them. It needs the real dates: entries by 20 November, prize-giving on 1 December."
hint: "Look for places where Sarah expects Claude to read her mind."
:::

::: step explain
The draft is ready. From here, there are three ways to change the document:

- **Yourself:** click into the text and type, everything saves automatically.
- **In the chat:** “Shorten the intro and add a section on prizes.” Claude makes the change and briefly says what it changed.
- **With a comment:** select some text, leave a comment and mention {{data.ui_paths.a1_mention_claude}}. Claude replies in the thread and makes the edit.

When it's all done, click {{data.ui_paths.a1_export}}: {{data.limits.a1_docs_export_formats}}.
:::

::: step explain
You can share a document: {{data.ui_paths.a1_share}}. There are two access levels: {{data.ui_paths.a1_docs_viewers}} and {{data.ui_paths.a1_docs_editors}}. The recipient needs a Claude account; {{data.plans.a1_link_sharing_personal}}, you share via a link.

Two things worth remembering:

- **Deleting is permanent.** There's no trash, and a deleted document can't be recovered.
- **Charts don't refresh on their own.** If Claude added a chart from data, ask it to update the chart when the data changes.
:::

::: exercise write_prompt
id: "a1.2.e5"
type: "write_prompt"
question: "Write a prompt to create a document in Claude Docs for a real task of yours. Say what the document is for, who will read it and which sections it needs. Don't include real phone numbers, addresses or other people's personal data."
placeholder: "Make a document: … It will be read by … Sections needed …"
ideas:
  - "What's the document for, and what should happen after people read it?"
  - "Who are the readers, and what do they already know?"
  - "Which sections, in what order?"
  - "Which facts do only you know: dates, amounts, rules?"
  - "How long, and in what tone?"
  - "Which format will you export to: Word, PDF, Google Docs?"
min_chars: 100
hint: "Answer the three questions from the help centre in your prompt: what the document is for, who will read it and what should be in it. Add facts only you know, so Claude doesn't make them up."
review:
  task: "An Advanced-level learner is writing a prompt to create a document in Claude Docs for a real task of theirs (a guide, rules, a plan, a job description, etc.). The lesson teaches: say what the document is for, who will read it and what it should cover (sections), and give facts Claude can't know (dates, amounts, rules) so it doesn't make them up. A strong prompt lets Claude write a draft with almost no clarifying questions. Section structure and tone are a big plus. Don't require mentions of export or the interface."
  criteria: ["R1", "R2", "R3", "R4"]
  reference_prompt: |
    Make a document: a guide for the new receptionist at our dental practice in Limerick. They'll read it on their first day, so that within a week they can manage bookings and greet patients on their own without constantly asking questions.

    The reader is a receptionist with no medical background who is comfortable with computers.

    Sections in this order:
    1. Shift pattern (Mon–Fri 8:00–20:00, Sat 9:00–15:00, 6-hour shifts).
    2. Booking patients: by phone and online, no bookings less than 2 hours ahead.
    3. What to do if a patient is more than 15 minutes late.
    4. Payment: cash and card; payment plans are set up only by the senior receptionist.
    5. Who to call in a difficult situation.

    Friendly tone, plain language, short paragraphs and lists. Length: 2–3 pages. If a section is missing information, add a note saying “check with Orla” instead of making something up. At the end I'll export it to PDF and print it.
  show_reference: true
  fallback_feedback:
    - "Is it clear what the document is for and what should happen after people read it?"
    - "Does it say who will read it and what they already know?"
    - "Are the sections, or at least the topics to cover, listed?"
    - "Does it include facts only you know: dates, amounts, rules?"
    - "Does it say what to do when information is missing: add a note rather than make things up?"
    - "Are the tone and approximate length specified?"
    - "Is the text free of real phone numbers, addresses and other people's personal data?"

::: step summary
**Claude Docs is a document you write together: you, Claude and your colleagues.**

In your prompt, say what the document is for, who's reading it and what goes in it. Fix small things yourself, ask for big changes in the chat and use comments for targeted ones. Export the finished document: {{data.limits.a1_docs_export_formats}}. And remember: deleting is permanent.

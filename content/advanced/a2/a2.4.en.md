::: step hook
Anna is a project manager at a construction company in Utrecht. She needs to present the quarter's results to the director. She writes:

```prompt
Make a presentation on the quarter's results
```

Claude sent 18 slides: company history, mission, “our values”… The quarter's figures don't appear until slide 12. The director has 10 minutes for the whole thing.
:::

::: step explain
Claude didn't know the most important thing: *who will read it, why, and how much time they have*.

A document is like a made-to-measure suit: a tailor with no measurements will make a “standard size”. A document's measurements are its audience, goal, structure, length and formatting. The more precise the measurements, the fewer the alterations.
:::

::: step explain
First, pick the format:

- **Word (.docx)** — text that people will edit, comment on and add to;
- **PowerPoint (.pptx)** — something shown on screen while you talk;
- **PDF** — the final version, which should look the same for everyone: to send to a client, print, or attach to an application.

Claude can also convert one format into another.
:::

::: exercise match_pairs
id: "a2.4.e1"
type: "match_pairs"
question: "Match each task with the file format it's best to ask for."
pairs:
  - left: "A draft bonus policy that the lawyer and the accountant will still edit"
    right: "Word (.docx)"
    why: "Several people will add to and comment on the document. That's exactly what Word is made for."
  - left: "A 10-minute update for the director at a team meeting"
    right: "PowerPoint (.pptx)"
    why: "It's a talk: the key figures are on screen and you say the rest. A .pptx file can be polished before you present."
  - left: "A sales proposal going to a client by email"
    right: "PDF"
    why: "The client sees the document exactly as you made it, on any device, and can't accidentally shift anything."
  - left: "A long report that needs turning into slides"
    right: "Attach the report first, then ask for a PowerPoint based on it"
    why: "Claude can convert a document from one format to another. That way the slides are based on your text, not on generalities."
hint: "Ask yourself: will the document be edited, presented, or sent as a finished piece?"
:::

::: step explain
The five measurements of any document:

1. **Audience**: who's reading and what they already know.
2. **Goal**: what the reader should understand or do afterwards.
3. **Structure**: sections or slides in order.
4. **Length**: pages, slides, minutes of speaking.
5. **Formatting and tone**: style, font, colours, formal or more relaxed.
:::

  - text: "Make a PowerPoint presentation "
    weak: false
    why: "The format is named directly. Claude will make a .pptx file, not text in the chat."
  - text: "for the director: "
    weak: false
    why: "The audience is named. She could add what the director wants from the update, but this is already good."
  - text: "Q3 results for our three sites from the attached report. "
    weak: false
    why: "The content and data source are clear: Claude will work from the report rather than invent figures."
  - text: "A normal length. "
    weak: true
    why: "Everyone's idea of “normal” is different — that's exactly how 18 slides happened. Better: “6–7 slides, for a 10-minute slot”."
  - text: "Make it look impressive somehow."
    weak: true
    why: "Claude will interpret “impressive” its own way. Describe what to do: “a clean, formal style in the company colours, navy and white, with one main takeaway in each slide's title”."
hint: "The audience and content are already there. Look for places where Claude has to guess the measurements."
:::

::: exercise build_prompt
id: "a2.4.e3"
type: "build_prompt"
question: "Build a prompt for a Word document: Anna is preparing a briefing sheet for new site managers. Leave out the blocks you don't need."
blocks:
  - id: "b1"
    text: "Make a Word document: a health and safety briefing sheet for site managers who have just joined the company."
    role: "goal"
    why: "What to make, in what format and for whom — in one sentence."
  - id: "b2"
    text: "They're experienced builders but don't know our internal rules. They'll read the sheet on their first day and keep it to hand on site."
    role: "context"
    why: "The audience and how the document will be used. From this it's clear: no construction basics, only our rules, easy to find what you need."
  - id: "b3"
    text: "Structure: 1) who to call if someone is injured, 2) required protective equipment, 3) rules for working at height, 4) what to do during a safety inspection. Use the rules from the attached procedures document."
    role: "format"
    why: "Sections in order and the source of the facts. Claude won't invent rules; it'll take them from the procedures."
  - id: "b4"
    text: "Length: up to 2 A4 pages, short bullet points, with key phone numbers and prohibitions in bold."
    role: "format"
    why: "Length and formatting are described as “what to do”. The document will be easy to print and pin up in the site cabin."
  - id: "b5"
    text: "Don't make it too long and boring."
    role: "noise"
    why: "A ban with no measure. The block about 2 pages and short points already says what to do — this one adds nothing."
  - id: "b6"
    text: "Feel free to add the history of health and safety around the world, it's interesting."
    role: "noise"
    why: "A site manager doesn't need this. Extra sections hide what matters — just like those 18 slides."
target_order: ["b1", "b2", "b3", "b4"]
hint: "Order: what and for whom → how it'll be used → sections → length and formatting. Skip the bans and the “interesting” extras."
:::

::: exercise single_choice
id: "a2.4.e4"
type: "single_choice"
question: "Claude has made Anna's PowerPoint. What's the sensible next step?"
options:
  - text: "Open the file, check the figures against the report and adjust the slides to suit her — tweak the wording, add photos of the sites"
    correct: true
    why: "Yes. Claude makes a good foundation; the final polish and checking the figures are up to you. After all, you're the one presenting, not Claude."
  - text: "Send it straight to the director without opening it: Claude took the data from the report, after all"
    correct: false
    why: "Even with the report to hand, a figure can get mixed up or rounded wrongly. Before showing it to the director, always open it and check."
  - text: "If she doesn't like something, ask Claude to “redo everything” without explaining"
    correct: false
    why: "Without an explanation, Claude doesn't know what to fix and may ruin what was good. It also eats into your limit. Better to be specific: “on slide 4, keep only three figures”."
hint: "Who's responsible for the presentation in front of the director?"
:::

::: step explain
Practise on your own task. The weak prompt below — replace it with a document from your own life or work: a report, a briefing sheet, a grant application, a CV, a talk.

Don't forget all five measurements. Navyk will send your prompt to Claude for feedback.
:::

The lesson teaches: choose a format to suit the task (editing — Word, presenting — PowerPoint, sending as a finished piece — PDF) and give five measurements: audience and goal (R1, R2), structure — sections or slides in order (R3), length — pages, slides, minutes (R3), formatting and tone phrased as “what to do” (R3). ::: step summary
**Without measurements, you get a “standard size”.** Pick the format: to edit — Word, to present — PowerPoint, to send as a finished piece — PDF.

Give the five measurements: audience, goal, structure, length, formatting. Then open the finished file, check it and give it the final polish yourself.

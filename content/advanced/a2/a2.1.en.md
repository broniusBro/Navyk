::: step hook
Liam manages the shift rota at a small car wash in Cork. He asks Claude:

```prompt
Make a November shift rota for 4 car washers
```

```claude
Here's the rota as a table:
| Date | Shift 1 | Shift 2 |
|---|---|---|
| 1 November | Sean | Conor |
…
```

The table is in the chat, but Liam needs a file he can open in Excel and print. Copy 30 rows by hand?
:::

::: step explain
No need. Claude has a *working environment*: its own separate “sandbox” computer where it writes and runs code. That's how it makes real files: Excel, Word, PowerPoint, PDF and chart images.

Think of a craftsman's tool shed: without it, he can only give advice; with it, he builds the thing and hands it to you.
:::

::: step explain
This is switched on by the {{data.ui_paths.a2_code_execution_toggle}} toggle. You'll find it in {{data.ui_paths.a2_settings_capabilities}}. On your phone, it's under {{data.ui_paths.a2_mobile_capabilities}}.

Usually you don't need to do anything: {{data.plans.a2_file_creation_default}}. Can't see the toggle on your work account? The owner controls it in {{data.ui_paths.a2_org_capabilities}}.
:::

::: step explain
A few small but important things:

- you can download the finished file with the {{data.ui_paths.a2_download}} button;
- file size: {{data.limits.a2_file_size}};
- working with files uses more of your limit than a regular chat: you can see what's left in {{data.ui_paths.settings_usage}}.

So describe the file precisely up front, rather than correcting it ten times in a row.
:::

::: step explain
Now for the key skill of this module: describing a file so it comes out right the first time.

Claude's help centre advises being specific about three things: *structure* (sheets, columns, sections), *content* (what data) and *formatting*. Try it on Liam's prompt.
:::

  task: "The learner improves the prompt “Make a November shift rota for 4 car washers” to get a file (usually Excel) instead of a table in the chat. The lesson teaches: name the file format directly (R1) and be specific about structure, content and formatting (R3); give the necessary facts — shift rules, hours, days off (R2). It's good if they say what to calculate (for example, hours per person) and what counts as a good result (R4). Details can be invented. Don't require formulas as a mandatory point: that's the next lesson's topic, but note it as a plus."
  criteria: ["R1", "R2", "R3", "R4"]
  reference_prompt: |
    Make an Excel file with the shift rota for November 2026 for a car wash. Washers: Sean, Conor, Dan, Ollie. The car wash is open every day from 8:00 to 20:00, with two shifts: 8:00–14:00 and 14:00–20:00, one person per shift. Everyone needs at least 2 days off a week, and nobody works both shifts on the same day.
    Structure: rows are the days of the month with the weekday, columns are “Shift 1” and “Shift 2”. On the right, a separate small table: how many shifts and hours each person works in the month.
    Formatting for printing on one A4 page: shade days off in light grey and freeze the header row.
  show_reference: true
  fallback_feedback:
    - "Have you named the file format: Excel, Word, PDF?"
    - "Have you described the structure: rows, columns, sheets?"
    - "Are the rules only you know included: opening hours, people per shift, days off?"
    - "Have you said what to calculate: for example, shifts and hours per person?"
    - "Have you specified the formatting: printing, highlighted days off, one page?"

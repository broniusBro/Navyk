::: step hook
Aoife is opening a café in Galway and writes to Claude:

```prompt
Find suppliers of coffee cups.
```

Half an hour later she has 40 links, half of them from another country, with some prices per cup and some per box. Claude did honest work. It just had its own idea of “done”.
:::

::: step explain
Hand off a big task the way you'd brief a sharp new hire who doesn't know how you do things. The brief has three parts:

- *result*: what exactly you want and in what form;
- *“done” criteria*: how to check the work is finished;
- *boundaries*: what can be done without asking and what only after your “yes”.

If order matters, add the steps as a list.
:::

::: exercise build_prompt
id: "a6.2.e1"
type: "build_prompt"
question: "Build Aoife's task from the blocks. Leave out the blocks you don't need."
blocks:
  - id: "b1"
    text: "Find 5 suppliers of 250 ml and 350 ml paper cups that deliver in Ireland and put a comparison into a table."
    role: "goal"
    why: "The result is specific: how many suppliers, what product, where, and what form the output should take."
  - id: "b2"
    text: "I'm opening a café that serves about 60 cups a day, so minimum order and price per 1,000 cups matter, not just the price per box."
    role: "context"
    why: "Context with a reason. Knowing the café's volume, Claude will filter out suppliers that only sell by the pallet."
  - id: "b3"
    text: "It's done when every supplier has a price per 1,000 cups, a minimum order, a delivery time and a link to the page where you found it. If you can't find something, write “no data”."
    role: "format"
    why: "These are the “done” criteria: both Claude and Aoife can check the table against them. “No data” is more honest than a made-up price."
  - id: "b4"
    text: "Don't contact suppliers or submit any enquiries: show me the table first."
    role: "format"
    why: "A boundary: anything other people will see happens only after Aoife agrees."
  - id: "b5"
    text: "This is a really important task, so try your very best!"
    role: "noise"
    why: "Sounds upbeat, but doesn't say what to do. Specific criteria work better than exclamation marks."
  - id: "b6"
    text: "While you're at it, come up with a name and logo for the café."
    role: "noise"
    why: "That's a different task. Extra requests blur the result; unrelated work is better in a separate conversation."
target_order: ["b1", "b2", "b3", "b4"]
hint: "First what to get, then why, then how to check it and what not to do without asking. Set aside vague blocks and other tasks."
:::

::: step explain
The most common mistake is a fuzzy “done”. “Find suppliers” doesn't say when to stop.

Good criteria are ones you can check by eye: “the table has 5 rows”, “every price has a link”, “the file opens in Excel”.

Add at the end: “Before you finish, check the result against these criteria.” That way Claude catches its own mistakes.
:::

::: exercise find_error
id: "a6.2.e2"
type: "find_error"
question: "Ben from Cork asks Claude to tidy up his documents. Tap every weak spot."
prompt: "Sort the scans in the “Documents 2026” folder into subfolders: invoices, contracts, certificates. Rename the files using the format “date_type_name”. Delete anything unnecessary. Make it look nice."
segments:
  - text: "Sort the scans in the “Documents 2026” folder into subfolders: invoices, contracts, certificates. "
    weak: false
    why: "Good: it's clear which folder to work with and which subfolders are needed."
  - text: "Rename the files using the format “date_type_name”. "
    weak: false
    why: "A specific naming format. Claude won't have to guess, and Ben can easily check the result."
  - text: "Delete anything unnecessary. "
    weak: true
    why: "A dangerous boundary: Claude decides what's “unnecessary”, and deleting can't be undone. Better: “put duplicates and unclear files in a ‘To check’ folder and I'll look at them myself”."
  - text: "Make it look nice."
    weak: true
    why: "You can't check that. What does “nice” mean? Better a criterion: “no files left loose in the main folder; at the end, show me a list of what you moved where”."
hint: "Look for things that can't be checked and things that can't be undone."
:::

::: step explain
Boundaries are about *reversibility*. Moving a file or writing a draft can be undone. Deleting, sending an email, paying or publishing can't.

Say it plainly: “do reversible things yourself; irreversible things only after I agree”.

And say what you want: “do” or “suggest”. If you write “suggest what to change”, Claude may only suggest.
:::

::: exercise multi_choice
id: "a6.2.e3"
type: "multi_choice"
question: "Which of these should Claude do only after you agree? Select all that apply."
options:
  - text: "Send emails to ten clients"
    correct: true
    why: "A sent email can't be taken back, and other people see it. Show the drafts first."
  - text: "Delete old versions of files"
    correct: true
    why: "Deleting can't be undone. It's safer to move them into a separate folder and decide yourself."
  - text: "Pay a supplier's invoice"
    correct: true
    why: "Money is irreversible. That decision is always yours."
  - text: "Create a draft spreadsheet with calculations"
    correct: false
    why: "A draft breaks nothing and is easy to fix. Claude can do this on its own, or you'll drown in approvals."
  - text: "Read files in a folder you connected yourself"
    correct: false
    why: "Reading changes nothing, and you gave access to the folder yourself. That's a normal part of the work."
hint: "Can it be undone? Will anyone other than you see it?"
:::

::: exercise single_choice
id: "a6.2.e4"
type: "single_choice"
question: "Ben wrote: “Can you suggest how to organise my documents?” Claude sent a plan but didn't move anything. Why?"
options:
  - text: "Ben asked for a suggestion, and Claude suggested"
    correct: true
    why: "Claude takes words literally. If you want action, say so: “organise the documents according to this plan”."
  - text: "Claude can't access files under any circumstances"
    correct: false
    why: "With a connected folder, Claude can move files. It's about how the task was worded."
  - text: "Claude is lazy and does the bare minimum"
    correct: false
    why: "Claude did exactly what it was asked. A plan instead of action comes from the word “suggest”."
hint: "Reread the verb in Ben's request."
:::

::: exercise write_prompt
id: "a6.2.e5"
type: "write_prompt"
question: "Describe your big task for Claude: result, context, “done” criteria and boundaries. If order matters, add steps."
placeholder: "I need you to prepare…"
ideas:
  - "What exactly should come out of it, and in what form?"
  - "Why is it needed, and who will use the result?"
  - "How will you know it's all done?"
  - "What can Claude do on its own, and what only after you agree?"
  - "What should happen if data is missing?"
min_chars: 150
hint: "Run the new-hire test: would a sharp assistant know, without asking, what to hand in, how to check it and what not to touch?"
review:
  task: "The learner writes a brief for a long multi-step task that Claude carries out on its own with tools (files, browser, connectors). The learner picks the task. The lesson teaches three parts: a specific result and its form; “done” criteria that can be checked (plus a request to check the result against them before finishing); boundaries: Claude does reversible things itself, irreversible ones (deleting, sending, paying, publishing) only after the user agrees. Steps are needed only if order matters. Don't mark down for missing XML tags or roles. Bonus: what to do if data is missing (“no data”, ask)."
  criteria: ["R1", "R2", "R3", "R4", "R5"]
  reference_prompt: |
    Prepare a comparison of three office rental offers in Dublin. The PDFs with the offers are in the connected “Office” folder.

    Why: on Friday I'm presenting the options to our director. There are 12 of us, and the budget is up to €9,000 a month including all charges.

    What to do:
    1. From each PDF, pull out the rent, floor area, service charges, lease term and break clause.
    2. Build an Excel table: one row per offer, columns for these items plus the total monthly cost.
    3. Under the table, write 3–5 sentences on which option is better value and what the risks are.

    It's done when every figure has the PDF page number next to it, the total monthly cost is calculated with a formula, and the table opens in Excel. If an item isn't in the PDF, write “not in the document” and don't fill it in yourself. Before you finish, check the table against these conditions.

    Boundaries: don't change or delete the PDFs, and don't contact the landlords. Save the result as a new file in the same folder.
  show_reference: true
  fallback_feedback:
    - "Is it clear what result is needed and in what form: a table, a document, a folder?"
    - "Does it explain why it's needed and who will use the result?"
    - "Are there “done” criteria you can check by eye?"
    - "Did you ask Claude to check the result against those criteria before finishing?"
    - "Does it say what Claude can do on its own and what (deleting, sending, paying) only after you agree?"
    - "If order matters, are there steps in a list?"
    - "Does it say what to do if data is missing?"
    - "Is the text free of real names, phone numbers and account numbers?"

::: step summary
**Big task = result + “done” criteria + boundaries.**

Criteria should be checkable by eye; ask Claude to check against them before finishing. Claude does reversible things on its own, irreversible ones after your “yes”. If you want action, say “do”, not “suggest”.

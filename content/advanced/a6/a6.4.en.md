::: step hook
Maddie in Kilkenny wants Claude to fill in her application for a stall at a craft fair on the organiser's website. There's no connector for that site. How will Claude get there?

There are three routes: the built-in browser, your Chrome, and control of your whole computer. Each has its own strengths and its own risks.
:::

::: step explain
Claude picks the most precise route:

1. *Connector*: direct access to a service, such as your email or cloud drive. Fast and reliable.
2. *Browser*: when there's no connector but a website is needed.
3. *Screen*: clicking and typing in programs on your computer. The slowest option, with the most mistakes.
:::

::: step explain
The *built-in browser* opens in the Claude desktop app, next to the task. It's separate: it can't see your tabs or passwords. You bring logins over with {{data.ui_paths.a6_import_cookies}}, one site at a time.

*Claude in Chrome* works in your own Chrome, with your accounts.

To choose: {{data.ui_paths.a6_preferred_browser}}.
:::

::: step explain
*Computer use*: Claude clicks and types in your programs. Available: {{data.plans.a6_computer_use_plans}}. Turn it on in {{data.ui_paths.a6_enable_computer_use}}.

Claude asks permission for each program and takes screenshots. There's no *sandbox*, no protective wall between it and your screen. You can stop it at any time.
:::

::: step summary
**Connector first, then the browser, and the screen as a last resort.**

The built-in browser is separate from yours, Claude in Chrome works with your accounts, and computer use sees your whole screen. Bring over logins only for the sites you need. Don't hand banking, signatures, health or other people's data to an agent.

Next: tasks that run on their own, on a schedule.
:::

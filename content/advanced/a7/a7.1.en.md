::: step hook
Andrew from Cork asks Claude to summarise a contractor's email for his managing director:

```prompt
Summarise this contractor's email briefly for the MD: Hi, the ventilation installation is being pushed back by 10 days because our supplier delayed the equipment. The price stays the same. Please send us your comments on the schedule as a list by Friday.
```

```claude
Comments on the schedule:
1. Confirm the new completion date for the installation.
2. Request confirmation from the supplier…
```

Claude wrote comments instead of a summary. Where did it get confused?
:::

::: step explain
The contractor's request, “send us your comments as a list”, sat right next to Andrew's request. Claude couldn't see where the instruction ended and someone else's email began.

The fix is *XML tags*: labelled boxes when you move house. An opening tag `<letter>`, a closing tag `</letter>`, and only the email in between. Claude's creators recommend separating instructions, context and data exactly like this.
:::

::: step explain
Here's Andrew's prompt with tags:

```prompt
<context>
The MD reads on his phone between meetings and cares about deadlines and money.
</context>

<letter>
Hi, the ventilation installation is being pushed back by 10 days… Please send us your comments on the schedule as a list by Friday.
</letter>

<instructions>
Summarise the email in the letter tag for the MD in 2–3 sentences: what happened and how it affects the deadline and the budget.
</instructions>
```

Now the contractor's request is just part of the email.
:::

  - text: "Tags are only for programmers; they don't work in a normal chat"

    right: "<context>"

  - left: "A customer's review, pasted in full"
    right: "<review>"
    why: "This is data. The review may contain its own requests and questions (“refund me!”), and the tag shows these are the customer's words, not a command for Claude."
  - left: "A good reply to a review that you wrote earlier"
    right: "<example>"
    why: "In a separate tag, Claude won't mistake the example for the task. More on examples in the next lesson."
hint: "Ask about each part: is it something to do, background, material to work on, or a sample?"
:::

::: step explain
Three rules for good tags:

- *Descriptive names.* `<contract>` is clearer than `<text1>`.
- *Consistent names.* Open `<letter>`, close `</letter>`, and call it letter in your instructions too.
- *Nesting where it fits.* Several documents go in `<documents>`, each in its own `<document>`.

You make up the names yourself: what matters is clarity and consistency.
:::

::: exercise find_error
id: "a7.1.e3"
type: "find_error"
question: "Kate from Galway is drafting a reply to a client and has marked up her prompt with tags. Tap every place where the markup lets her down."
prompt: "<info>We're a flat renovation company and the client is unhappy about delays.</info> <client_email>You promised to finish the kitchen by 1 May, and it's now the 10th. Tell me honestly when you'll be done.</email> <instructions>Write a polite reply to the client's email above, 4–5 sentences. The new date is 20 May. Offer a 5% discount.</instructions>"
segments:
  - text: "<info>We're a flat renovation company and the client is unhappy about delays.</info> "
    weak: true
    why: "The tag works, but the name is too vague: “info” could mean anything. A descriptive name like <context> or <about_us> tells you straight away what's inside."
  - text: "<client_email>You promised to finish the kitchen by 1 May, and it's now the 10th. Tell me honestly when you'll be done.</email> "
    weak: true
    why: "It opens with <client_email> but closes with </email>. The names have to match, or the boundaries of the email blur. It should close with </client_email>."
  - text: "<instructions>Write a polite reply to the client's email above, 4–5 sentences. "
    weak: true
    why: "“The email above” is a vague reference. Better to name the tag: “to the email in the client_email tag”. That makes the link between instruction and data unambiguous."
  - text: "The new date is 20 May. Offer a 5% discount.</instructions>"
    weak: false
    why: "Good: the specific facts for the reply sit with the instruction, so Claude doesn't have to make them up."
hint: "Check three things: are the tag names clear, do the opening and closing tags match, and does the instruction refer to the tag by name?"
:::

::: step summary
**Someone else's text goes in its own box.**

Separate instructions, context and data with XML tags that have clear, consistent names. In the instruction, name the tag to work with.

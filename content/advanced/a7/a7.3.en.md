::: step hook
Olivia from Dublin is choosing a contractor to fit out her office. She pasted a question into the chat, followed by three contractor proposals in a row, with no labels:

```prompt
Who's cheapest and fastest? [9-page proposal] [12-page proposal] [7-page proposal]
```

```claude
The second proposal is the cheapest at €41,000, and the first company will finish fastest…
```

Olivia checked: €41,000 is the price from the third proposal. What went wrong?
:::

::: step explain
Two problems.

First: *the question came first*, and by the end of the long texts it gets “buried”. Anthropic recommends the opposite: documents at the top, question and instructions at the bottom. In their tests this improved answer quality by up to 30%, especially with several documents.

Second: the documents *have no labels*, so it's easy for Claude to mix up which is which.
:::

id: "a7.3.e1"
type: "order_steps"
question: "Put the parts of Olivia's prompt in the right order, top to bottom."
shuffle: true
items:
  - text: "The three proposals, each in its own tag with a label"
    why: "Long data goes at the very top. Claude reads it first and meets the question at the end, with the material already in view."
  - text: "Context: what the office is and what matters most to Olivia"
    why: "Context comes after the documents, closer to the question. It explains which angle to look at the proposals from."
  - text: "Instruction: what to compare and what form the answer should take"
    why: "Instructions and examples go below the documents. That's what the long-context guide recommends."
  - text: "The question itself: “Which proposal is the best deal for us, and why?”"
    why: "The question goes at the very end. This is the placement that improved quality in Anthropic's tests."
hint: "One rule: everything long goes up, everything that says what to do with it goes down. The question comes last."
:::

::: step explain
Labels are made with tags. Each document goes in its own `<document>`, with the source and the text inside:

```prompt
<documents>
<document index="1">
<source>Proposal from Rapid Fitout, 3 March</source>
<document_content>
…proposal text…
</document_content>
</document>
<document index="2">
…
</document>
</documents>
```

`<source>` is *metadata*: who, what, when. Like the label on a folder in an archive.
:::

::: exercise match_pairs
id: "a7.3.e2"
type: "match_pairs"
question: "Match each tag with what goes inside it."
pairs:
  - left: "<documents>"
    right: "All the documents together, in one stack"
    why: "The outer box. Nesting shows there are several documents with the same purpose inside."
  - left: '<document index="2">'
    right: "One specific document with its own number"
    why: "The number lets you refer to it: “compare documents 1 and 2”. You and Claude are talking about the same thing."
  - left: "<source>"
    right: "Where the document came from: company, author, date"
    why: "Metadata. Exactly what Olivia was missing: without labels, Claude attributed the price to the wrong company."
  - left: "<document_content>"
    right: "The document text itself, unchanged"
    why: "The text is kept apart from the label. Claude won't confuse “proposal of 3 March” with what the proposal says."
hint: "Go from big to small: the whole stack, one document, its label, its text."
:::

::: exercise single_choice
id: "a7.3.e3"
type: "single_choice"
question: "Tim from Waterford is comparing three versions of a lease: the landlord's, his solicitor's and the final one. What's most important to put in each version's <source>?"
options:
  - text: "Who prepared the version and what date it's from"
    correct: true
    why: "That's exactly what tells the versions apart. Then Claude can say: “in the solicitor's version of 12 May, clause 4.2 was changed”."
  - text: "How many pages the document has"
    correct: false
    why: "That doesn't help you see how the versions differ or which one is current. Choose metadata to suit the question."
  - text: "Nothing, Claude will work out from the text which version is which"
    correct: false
    why: "Three near-identical texts are the best way to mix everything up. That's exactly what happened to Olivia."
  - text: "The landlord's passport details, to be more precise"
    correct: false
    why: "You don't need a passport to compare versions, and it's better to remove personal data before sending. Metadata is a label for the document, not a file on a person."
hint: "Ask yourself: what label would help Claude tell one version from another and refer to it?"
:::

::: step explain
One last touch from the guide: with long documents, ask Claude to *pull out the relevant quotes first*, then answer. That way it focuses on what matters and isn't distracted by the rest.

::: step summary
**Long stuff up top, question at the bottom, a label on every document.**

Wrap documents in `<document>` with `<source>` and `<document_content>`, number them and ask your question last.

Next: how to make the answer rest on quotes and remove anything that isn't backed up.

::: step hook
Rachel from Athlone is applying for a project manager job:

```prompt
Write a cover letter for this job and check yourself so it's perfect.
```

```claude
Done! I've double-checked the letter: it's persuasive and fully matches the job.
```

But the ad asked for experience with Jira and German, and the letter doesn't mention either. “Check yourself” didn't work. Why?
:::

::: step explain
Claude checked without knowing *what* to check against, and did it all inside a single reply.

Anthropic describes a reliable pattern, *self-correction*, in three steps:

1. **Draft.**
2. **Critique against criteria:** Claude compares the draft with a specific list.
3. **Revise:** Claude fixes the text based on that critique.

In the API these are three separate calls. In chat, three separate messages: you see the critique before the edits.
:::

::: exercise order_steps
id: "a7.5.e1"
type: "order_steps"
question: "Put the steps of the self-check for Rachel's letter in order."
shuffle: true
items:
  - text: "Ask for a draft of the letter, with the job ad attached"
    why: "First you need material to check. Don't demand perfection on the first try."
  - text: "Ask for a critique against a list: Jira, German, 3 facts from experience, under 200 words"
    why: "Criteria are the heart of the method. Without them, “check” turns into “praise”."
  - text: "Read the critique and drop any comments you disagree with"
    why: "This is why the steps are kept apart: you see the intermediate result and can step in before the edits."
  - text: "Ask Claude to rewrite the letter based on the remaining comments"
    why: "The revision rests on a specific critique, not on “make it better”."
hint: "You can't critique something that doesn't exist, and you can't revise based on a critique that hasn't been written yet."
:::

::: step explain
Two traps in critique.

**Without criteria**, Claude doesn't know what matters, so it praises.

**“Find as many flaws as possible”** is the opposite extreme. Claude's creators warn that a reviewer asked to look for problems will find them even in good work. Chase every one and the text bloats. Ask it to flag only what breaks the criteria.
:::

::: exercise single_choice
id: "a7.5.e2"
type: "single_choice"
question: "Which message is better for the “critique” step?"
options:
  - text: "“Check the letter against these criteria: Jira experience is mentioned; my level of German is stated; there are 3 facts from my experience with numbers; under 200 words. Flag only what breaks these points, one sentence each. Don't rewrite anything yet”"
    correct: true
    why: "It has specific criteria, a “violations only” limit and a format for the critique. “Don't rewrite anything yet” keeps the steps separate: Rachel sees the comments first."
  - text: "“Check the letter and make it perfect”"
    correct: false
    why: "That's where Rachel started. No criteria, nothing to check against. And the check is mixed up with the edit straight away."
  - text: "“Find as many flaws in the letter as you can, at least 10”"
    correct: false
    why: "Claude will find 10 even if the letter is good. Some comments will be nitpicks, and editing based on them will spoil the text."
  - text: "“You're a strict recruiter. Rate the letter from 1 to 10”"
    correct: false
    why: "The role is fine, but a number without criteria tells you nothing: what exactly do you fix to get a 9?"
hint: "Look for the option with a specific list, a limit on comments and no immediate edit."
:::

::: exercise find_error
id: "a7.5.e3"
type: "find_error"
question: "Brian from Sligo is polishing a sales proposal for a client. Tap the weak spots in his critique message."
prompt: "Here's the draft proposal. Find every possible flaw, even small ones. Criteria: price and timeline are in the first paragraph, there are 2 case studies from similar clients, the tone is businesslike with no pressure. And rewrite it straight away taking all the comments into account."
segments:
  - text: "Here's the draft proposal. "
    weak: false
    why: "It's clear which text this is about."
  - text: "Find every possible flaw, even small ones. "
    weak: true
    why: "An invitation to nitpick. Claude will find small flaws even in a good text. Better: “flag only what breaks the criteria below”."
  - text: "Criteria: price and timeline are in the first paragraph, there are 2 case studies from similar clients, the tone is businesslike with no pressure. "
    weak: false
    why: "Excellent part: specific, checkable criteria. Each one gets a clear “yes” or “no”."
  - text: "And rewrite it straight away taking all the comments into account."
    weak: true
    why: "Critique and edit have merged into one step. Brian won't see the comments and can't drop the unnecessary ones before the text is rewritten."
hint: "One part is excellent. Look for what invites nitpicking and what glues the steps together."
:::

::: step explain
Not everything needs three messages.

- *Simple task:* one line at the end of the prompt is enough: “Before you finish, check your answer against these criteria: …”. That's what the guide suggests.
- *Important text:* three steps in separate messages.
- *Need an unbiased view:* run the critique in a new chat. Claude's creators note that with a fresh context it doesn't cling to what it has just written.
:::

::: exercise write_prompt
id: "a7.5.e5"
type: "write_prompt"
question: "Write a critique message for your draft: what the text is and who it's for, 3–5 specific criteria, “flag only violations of the criteria”, a format for the comments, and “don't rewrite yet”."
placeholder: "<draft>…</draft>\nCheck the draft against these criteria: …"
ideas:
  - "What the text is and who will read it"
  - "3–5 criteria you can answer “yes/no” to"
  - "Violations only, no nitpicking"
  - "Format: point → what's wrong → how to fix it"
  - "Don't rewrite yet"
  - "The draft in a tag"
min_chars: 120
hint: "Put the draft in a tag and replace the text with “…”. Below that, say who the text is for and what it should achieve, then 3–5 checkable criteria. Ask it to flag only violations, set the format for comments and ask it not to rewrite yet."
review:
  task: "Advanced level, lesson on self-correction “draft → critique → revise”. The learner writes the message for step two, the critique of their draft (the draft text may be replaced with “…”, which is fine). The core is R4: 3–5 specific, checkable criteria for a good text, and the limit “flag only what violates the criteria” (rather than “find all flaws”). R1: it's clear what the text is, who it's for and what it should achieve. R3: the critique format is set (e.g. “criterion → what's wrong → how to fix it”) and it says not to rewrite yet, so the steps stay separate. If the learner asks for a rewrite in the same message, flag that as a weak spot. Bonus: draft in a tag, suggestion to run the critique in a new chat."
  criteria: ["R1", "R3", "R4"]
  reference_prompt: |
    <draft>
    …
    </draft>

    This is a cover letter for a project manager role at a software company. It'll be read by a recruiter who spends a minute on each letter, so the key points need to be visible straight away.

    Check the draft in the draft tag against these criteria:
    1. The first paragraph says which role I'm applying for and why I'm a good fit.
    2. It mentions my experience with Jira, which the job requires.
    3. It states my level of German (B2) and where I use it.
    4. It has 3 facts from my experience with numbers.
    5. It's no longer than 200 words.

    Flag only what breaks these criteria, with no comments on style. One line per violation: “criterion number — what's wrong — how to fix it”. If a criterion is met, write “ok”. Don't rewrite the letter yet: I want to look at the comments first.
  show_reference: true
  fallback_feedback:
    - "Is it clear what the text is, who will read it and what it should achieve?"
    - "Are there 3–5 criteria you can answer “yes” or “no” to?"
    - "Do you ask it to flag only violations of the criteria, not “all flaws”?"
    - "Is the format for comments set: criterion, what's wrong, how to fix it?"
    - "Does it say not to rewrite yet, so you see the critique first?"
:::

::: step summary
**Draft → critique against criteria → revise. Three steps, not one.**

Give specific criteria, ask for violations only, and read the critique before the edits. For simple tasks, one line is enough: “before replying, check against these criteria…”.

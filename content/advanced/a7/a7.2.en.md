::: step hook
Dan handles customer support for an online shoe shop in Galway. He showed Claude one good reply, to a complaint about a late delivery. Now a customer is asking about sizing:

```claude
Hi there! We're very sorry your delivery was delayed. We've already contacted the courier… By the way, about sizing: this style runs half a size small.
```

Claude copied not just the tone but also the topic of the example. Why?
:::

::: step explain
One example is a narrow window. Claude doesn't know what matters in it: the tone, the length or the bit about delivery. So it copies everything.

Claude's creators call examples one of the most reliable ways to set format and tone. But examples should be *similar to the task*, *different from each other* and *kept separate* from the instruction. 3–5 work best.
:::

::: exercise single_choice
id: "a7.2.e1"
type: "single_choice"
question: "Dan wants to add two more examples to the late-delivery reply. Which set of three is best?"
options:
  - text: "A late delivery, a sizing question, a refund request for a faulty pair"
    correct: true
    why: "Three different situations from his real work. All they share is tone and format, and that's what Claude will pick up. Each reply has its own topic, so there's nothing to copy."
  - text: "A 2-day delay, a week-long delay, a delay over the holidays"
    correct: false
    why: "All three are about the same thing. Claude will decide every reply needs an apology for delivery, which is exactly the mistake from the start of the lesson."
  - text: "The late-delivery reply plus two nice New Year greetings to customers"
    correct: false
    why: "Greetings don't resemble the task: support answers questions and complaints. Examples like these will throw off the tone and length."
  - text: "The late-delivery reply, then the same reply twice more to be safe"
    correct: false
    why: "Repetition adds no information, it only strengthens the skew: Claude will be even more convinced that delivery is a required topic."
hint: "A good set resembles real customer questions, but each example is about something different."
:::

::: step explain
Separate examples with tags, as in the last lesson:

```prompt
<examples>
<example>
Question: My parcel is already 3 days late.
Reply: …
</example>
<example>
Question: What size should I get if I'm usually an 8?
Reply: …
</example>
</examples>
```

After the examples, say what matters in them: “take the tone and length; decide each reply's topic from the question.”
:::

::: exercise find_error
id: "a7.2.e2"
type: "find_error"
question: "Amy from Kilkenny wants photo captions for her yoga studio's page. Tap every weak spot in her examples."
prompt: "Write 5 photo captions for our yoga studio's Instagram. Here are some examples: “Mornings start with a stretch 🌿”, “Morning is the best time to practise 🌿”, “Morning yoga sets you up for the day 🌿”. Write the captions in the same spirit."
segments:
  - text: "Write 5 photo captions for our yoga studio's Instagram. "
    weak: false
    why: "The task is clear: how many, what for and what about."
  - text: "Here are some examples: "
    weak: true
    why: "The examples sit right inside the instruction. Better to move them into <examples> and wrap each in <example>, so Claude doesn't confuse samples with the task."
  - text: "“Mornings start with a stretch 🌿”, “Morning is the best time to practise 🌿”, “Morning yoga sets you up for the day 🌿”. "
    weak: true
    why: "All three are about mornings and use the same emoji. Claude will assume that's required, and all 5 captions will be about mornings with 🌿. She needs different occasions: an evening class, a beginner's first session, a workshop."
  - text: "Write the captions in the same spirit."
    weak: true
    why: "“In the same spirit” doesn't say what to take. Better to spell it out: “short, under 8 words, warm, one emoji; pick each topic from the photo”."
hint: "Check the examples against three rules: do they resemble the task, are they different from each other, and are they separate from the instruction? And does she say what matters in them?"
:::

::: exercise match_pairs
id: "a7.2.e3"
type: "match_pairs"
question: "Match each problem in Claude's replies with how to fix it in the examples."
pairs:
  - left: "Every reply starts with the same phrase"
    right: "Give your examples different openings"
    why: "Claude picks up patterns from examples, even accidental ones. If every example starts the same way, it'll treat that as a rule."
  - left: "Claude mixed up an example with the task and answered the question from the example"
    right: "Wrap the examples in <example> inside <examples>"
    why: "Tags show where the samples are and where the task is. That's what the guide means by “structured” examples."
  - left: "The replies read smoothly but don't fit your work"
    right: "Take examples from real situations in your task"
    why: "Examples should be close to the real use case. A sample from another field sets the wrong tone and length."
  - left: "Claude gets flustered by an unusual question"
    right: "Add an example with an edge case"
    why: "Diversity includes edge cases: a rude customer, an off-topic question, a request you can't fulfil. Show how to handle those too."
hint: "Each problem breaks one of the rules: similar to the task, different from each other, separated by tags."
:::

::: step explain
Where do you get 3 good examples? Your own past replies, emails and posts that worked well.

Not sure they're varied enough? Claude's creators suggest asking Claude itself: “rate my examples: how close are they to the task, and are they varied?” or “suggest two more examples with different situations”. Check any it writes yourself before pasting them in.
:::

::: exercise multi_choice

type: "multi_choice"
question: "Dan has collected 3 examples. What's sensible to do before sending the prompt? Select all that apply."
options:
  - text: "Ask Claude to assess whether the examples are varied and close to real questions"
    correct: true
    why: "This is direct advice from the guide: Claude can evaluate examples for relevance and diversity. A fresh pair of eyes spots a skew the author can't see."
  - text: "Ask Claude to write one more example, for a rude or odd question"
    correct: true
    why: "Claude can generate extra examples based on your set. An edge case makes the set more varied. Just reread it yourself."
  - text: "Say after the examples what to take from them: tone and length, but not the topic"
    correct: true
    why: "That way Claude doesn't have to guess what matters. It's the “why” from the Beginner level, applied to examples."
  - text: "Add 15 more similar delivery examples, because more is better"
    correct: false
    why: "The guide recommends 3–5 varied examples. Fifteen identical ones will only lock in the skew and take up space in the context."
hint: "Think about what makes a set varied and clear, and what just bloats it."
:::

::: exercise write_prompt
id: "a7.2.e5"
type: "write_prompt"
question: "Write a prompt for a task you do regularly: what to do, the context, and 3 different examples in <example> tags inside <examples>. Say what to take from the examples."
placeholder: "Write a product description for… <examples><example>…</example></examples>"
ideas:
  - "What the task is and who the result is for"
  - "3 different situations, not one three times"
  - "One example with an edge case"
  - "What to take from the examples: tone, length, structure"
  - "New data in a separate tag"
min_chars: 150
hint: "Start with the task and context. Then three examples: similar to your work, but about different things. Wrap each in <example> and all of them in <examples>. Finish by saying what to take from them and what data to work with now."
review:
  task: "Advanced level, lesson on examples (few-shot). The learner writes a prompt for a recurring task with three examples. The core is R6: there are 3 examples (3–5 is also fine); they resemble the real task but differ from each other (different situations, different openings, an edge case); they're wrapped in <example> inside <examples>; the prompt says what to take from the examples (tone, length, structure) and what to decide afresh. Also assess clarity of the task (R1) and format (R3). If all examples are about the same thing or start the same way, flag the risk that Claude will carry that into its reply. If the examples contain real customer names with contact details, R10 applies."
  criteria: ["R1", "R3", "R6"]
  reference_prompt: |
    I handle customer support for an online shoe shop. Write a reply to the customer's question in the question tag.

    <examples>
    <example>
    Question: My parcel is already 3 days late, where is it?
    Reply: I understand, waiting is frustrating. Your parcel was held up at the sorting centre and went out with the courier today, so it'll be with you tomorrow. I'll send the tracking number in my next message.
    </example>
    <example>
    Question: I'm usually a size 8, what size should I get in these trainers?
    Reply: This style runs half a size small, so we'd recommend an 8.5. If you're unsure, order both sizes: you can return the other one for free.
    </example>
    <example>
    Question: Do you actually answer messages or what?! This is day three!
    Reply: I'm sorry it's taken so long, that's our mistake. I'm on it now: send me your order number and I'll sort it out within the hour.
    </example>
    </examples>

    From the examples, take the tone (calm, polite, no corporate jargon) and the length (2–3 sentences, ending with a concrete next step). Decide the topic and facts of the reply from the question only. If you don't have enough information to answer, ask the customer for it.

    <question>
    Can I change the colour on my order if I placed it yesterday?
    </question>
  show_reference: true
  fallback_feedback:
    - "Are there exactly 3 examples (or 3–5), and do they resemble your real task?"
    - "Are the examples varied: situations, opening phrases, an edge case?"
    - "Is each example wrapped in <example>, and all of them in <examples>?"
    - "Does the prompt say what to take from the examples and what to decide afresh?"
    - "Is it clear what new data to work with now?"
:::

::: step summary
**Three different examples beat one, and beat ten identical ones.**

Take examples from your own task, make them varied, wrap them in `<example>` and say what to take from them. Not sure? Ask Claude to assess the set.

::: step hook
Sean, the marketer at a gym in Cork, pasted the brief for a new membership plan and asked for a news post for the website:

```claude
Meet our new Early Bird membership! Unlimited sessions from 7am to noon, a 25-metre pool — the only one in the area — and a free consultation with a trainer…
```

The brief doesn't say a word about a 25-metre pool or being “the only one in the area”. It sounds plausible, but it's made up.
:::

::: step explain
In the Beginner level you let Claude say “I don't know” and asked for quotes before the answer. Now let's put together the full working cycle from Anthropic's guide to reducing hallucinations:

1. *Quotes:* pull them out word for word and number them.
2. *Conclusion:* based only on those quotes, citing the number.
3. *Check:* match every claim in the draft to a quote. No quote, remove it.
:::

::: exercise order_steps
id: "a7.4.e1"
type: "order_steps"
question: "Put the steps of Sean's prompt in the right order."
shuffle: true
items:
  - text: "Pull out word-for-word quotes about the plan from the brief and number them. If something isn't there, say so"
    why: "Support comes first. Numbering lets you refer to quotes later, and “no quotes” is an honest way out instead of making things up."
  - text: "Write the news post using only these quotes, and put the quote number next to each fact"
    why: "The conclusion is built only on what was extracted. The number makes the answer checkable: you can see where each fact came from."
  - text: "Reread the post and find a quote for every claim"
    why: "This is the check after the draft. It catches facts that crept in “from memory” rather than from the brief."
  - text: "Delete claims without a quote and mark the spot with empty brackets []"
    why: "A technique from the guide: empty brackets show where an unsupported fact was. You can see what's gone and decide whether to add it back yourself."
hint: "Support → conclusion → check → clean-up. You can't check something that hasn't been written yet."
:::

::: step explain
Important: these techniques *significantly reduce* hallucinations but don't eliminate them entirely. Anthropic says so plainly.

So for high-stakes decisions (money, health, contracts), check critical figures and quotes against the document yourself. Quote numbers make that quick: open, find, compare.
:::

::: step summary
**Quotes first, then the conclusion, then a check of every claim.**

No quote, no claim: remove it and mark it with []. The technique cuts down on made-up details a lot, but not to zero, so check anything important yourself.

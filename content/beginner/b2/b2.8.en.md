::: step hook
Module test. Over seven lessons you've built up a toolkit:

- a clear goal and the colleague test;
- the “why” and context;
- describing the answer instead of banning things;
- steps in order;
- a one-sentence role;
- examples and corrections in conversation.

Now you'll combine them into one strong prompt: preparing for a job interview.
:::

::: exercise match_pairs
id: "b2.8.e1"
type: "match_pairs"
question: "Warm-up. Match each part of a prompt with what it gives Claude."
pairs:
  - left: "Role: “You're a recruiter who…”"
    right: "An angle and a tone for the answer"
    why: "The role tells Claude from what position to look at the task and how to speak."
  - left: "Goal: “Help me prepare for an interview”"
    right: "An understanding of what result is needed"
    why: "The colleague test starts with the goal: without it, it's unclear what to do at all."
  - left: "Context: “It's my first job after maternity leave, so I'm nervous about the gap”"
    right: "Facts and reasons only you know"
    why: "Context with a “so” or “because” helps Claude decide what to focus on."
  - left: "Format: “As a list, 5 questions, each with a 3–4 sentence sample answer”"
    right: "What the finished answer should look like"
    why: "A description of the answer (length, structure) instead of bans."
  - left: "Steps: “1. … 2. … 3. …”"
    right: "Order and completeness, so nothing gets lost"
    why: "Numbered steps are needed when there are several tasks and order matters."
hint: "Think back to what each lesson in the module was about."
:::

::: exercise build_prompt
id: "b2.8.e2"
type: "build_prompt"
question: "Build the complete prompt for Alina, who has a job interview coming up. Put the blocks in the best order and leave out the ones you don't need."
blocks:
  - id: "b1"
    text: "You're an HR manager at a pharmacy chain who interviews pharmacists."
    role: "role"
    why: "A role in the first sentence sets the angle for the whole prompt."
  - id: "b2"
    text: "Help me prepare for my interview for a senior pharmacist position at a pharmacy in Utrecht; it's on Thursday."
    role: "goal"
    why: "The goal right after the role: Claude understands the task and the deadline."
  - id: "b3"
    text: "I've worked as a pharmacist for 5 years but have never managed people, so I'm worried about questions on running a shift."
    role: "context"
    why: "Context with a reason: Claude will understand it should focus on questions about leadership."
  - id: "b4"
    text: "1. List the 5 most likely questions. 2. For each one, give a sample strong answer based on my experience. 3. At the end, give 3 questions I can ask them."
    role: "format"
    why: "Steps and the structure of the answer: what to do and in what order."
  - id: "b5"
    text: "Use plain language, with each sample answer 3–4 sentences long, so I can practise saying it out loud."
    role: "format"
    why: "Length and tone with a reason: the format is described as the result you want."
  - id: "b6"
    text: "Don't write rubbish and don't waffle."
    role: "noise"
    why: "Two bans with no description. The format you need is already set in the previous block, and this sentence adds nothing."
  - id: "b7"
    text: "By the way, do you know what the weather's like in Utrecht?"
    role: "noise"
    why: "An off-topic question. New task, new chat; here it would only distract from the interview."
target_order: ["b1", "b2", "b3", "b4", "b5"]
hint: "Role, goal, context, steps, format. Look for blocks with bans and off-topic questions."
:::

::: exercise find_error
id: "b2.8.e3"
type: "find_error"
question: "Tim wrote a prompt but failed several lessons from the module. Tap every weak spot."
prompt: "You're an expert. Prepare me for an interview at some company. Don't make it long and no clichés. And some stuff about salary too."
segments:
  - text: "You're an expert. "
    weak: true
    why: "A role with no focus: an expert in what, and what do they look at? Better: “You're a sales manager who hires sales reps”."
  - text: "Prepare me for an interview "
    weak: false
    why: "The goal is clear: preparing for an interview. This is a good part of the prompt."
  - text: "at some company. "
    weak: true
    why: "Fails the colleague test: which company, what position, when's the interview? Without that, the advice will be generic."
  - text: "Don't make it long and no clichés. "
    weak: true
    why: "Bans instead of a description. How many questions, how long should the answers be, in what form?"
  - text: "And some stuff about salary too."
    weak: true
    why: "A separate task tossed in at the end. Make it a step: “3. Tell me how to talk about my expected salary and what range to give”."
hint: "Check the prompt against each lesson: role, colleague test, format without bans, steps."
:::

    correct: true

  - text: "Start a new chat and do it all again"
    correct: false
    why: "Almost everything is already good, and there have been zero corrections. A new chat is for after two failed corrections or for a different task."
  - text: "Write “Redo it”"
    correct: false
    why: "Claude doesn't know what exactly is wrong and will rewrite everything. The good answers may get lost."
hint: "Remember the lesson on conversation: you like almost everything except one answer."

::: exercise write_prompt
id: "b2.8.e5"
type: "write_prompt"
question: "Write a complete prompt asking Claude to help you prepare for an interview for a specific position. Include a role, goal, context, steps and answer format."
placeholder: "You're… Help me prepare for an interview for…"
ideas:
  - "Role: who's doing the interview?"
  - "What position, and where?"
  - "When's the interview?"
  - "Your experience, and what worries you?"
  - "Steps: questions, answers, what to ask them"
  - "Length and tone of the answer"
min_chars: 100
hint: "Go in order: “You're…” (role), “Help me…” (goal and position), your situation with a “because” or “so”, numbered steps, and finally what the answer should look like. Run the colleague test before sending. Don't include real names or phone numbers."
review:
  task: "Module test for “How to write prompts”. The learner writes a complete prompt to prepare for an interview for a specific position. Expected: a one-sentence role (P7), a clear goal with the position (P1), context with reasons (P2), numbered steps (P4) and a format described as the desired result rather than as bans (P3). Assess the role and steps under R1 and R4. Praise each technique used and name the ones that are missing."
  criteria: ["R1", "R2", "R3", "R4"]
  reference_prompt: |
    You're a marketing manager who hires social media managers and values practical examples more than theory. Help me prepare for my interview for a social media manager position at a chain of gyms in Barcelona; it's on Monday. I have 2 years' experience: I ran a bakery's Instagram and grew it from 800 to 6,000 followers. I haven't worked in fitness before, so I'm worried about questions on that niche.
    1. List 6 questions I'm most likely to be asked, including some about a fitness audience.
    2. For each one, give a sample strong answer based on my experience, 3–4 sentences.
    3. Suggest 3 questions I can ask them.
    Keep it friendly and in plain language, so the answers are easy to say out loud.
  show_reference: true
  fallback_feedback:
    - "Role: is there a “You're…” sentence that says what this person looks at?"
    - "Goal: is it clear you need help preparing for an interview for a specific position?"
    - "Context: does it include your experience, the company, the date and at least one “because” or “so”?"
    - "Steps: are the tasks split into numbered points?"
    - "Format: are length, structure and tone set, without bans like “don't write…”?"
    - "Colleague test: could someone with no context help you based on this prompt?"
:::

::: step summary
**A strong prompt: role, goal, context with the “why”, steps, format.**

Not every task needs all of it at once: for a simple question, one clear sentence is enough. But the more the result matters, the more tools are worth using. And if the answer isn't quite right, keep the conversation going.

Module complete!
:::

::: step hook
Tom is selling his son's old bike and writes to Claude:

```prompt
Write an ad to sell a bike
```

```claude
Bike for sale in excellent condition! Perfect for getting around town and weekend adventures. Sturdy frame, smooth ride, comfortable saddle. Price negotiable. Call or message me, happy to answer any questions!
```

It reads smoothly, but it would fit any bike in the world. Why do you think that happened?
:::

::: step explain
Imagine a very smart new colleague has just joined your team. They learn fast and can do anything, but on day one they know nothing about how things work at your place.

Claude is the same. Anthropic, the company behind Claude, actually recommends thinking of it this way. If you didn't write it, Claude doesn't know it. It'll fill the gaps with generic filler.
:::

::: exercise compare_answers
id: "b2.1.e1"
type: "compare_answers"
variant: "why_better"
question: "Tom rewrote his prompt. Why is the second answer more useful?"
cards:
  - id: "A"
    label: "Before"
    prompt: "Write an ad to sell a bike"
    answer: |
      Bike for sale in excellent condition! Perfect for getting around town and weekend adventures. Sturdy frame, smooth ride, comfortable saddle. Price negotiable. Call or message me, happy to answer any questions!
  - id: "B"
    label: "After"
    prompt: "Write a Facebook Marketplace ad: I'm selling a kids' bike, 20-inch wheels, for heights 120–140 cm. Bought in 2023, my son has outgrown it. Chain replaced a month ago, small scratch on the frame. €80, open to small offers. Collection only, Rathmines, Dublin. 5–6 lines, honest and to the point."
    answer: |
      Kids' bike, 20″ wheels — fits heights 120–140 cm
      Bought in 2023, selling because my son has outgrown it.
      Good condition: new chain fitted a month ago, rides smoothly. Small scratch on the frame (shown in photos).
      Price: €80, open to small offers.
      Collection only: Rathmines, Dublin.
      Message me with any questions and I'll send more photos.
options:
  - text: "The second prompt has facts only Tom knows, and Claude was able to use them"
    correct: true
    why: "Exactly. Size, age, chain, scratch, price, area. Claude couldn't have guessed any of it. As soon as the facts were in the prompt, they made it into the ad, and buyers don't need to ask."
  - text: "The second prompt is longer, and Claude always does better with long prompts"
    correct: false
    why: "It's not about length. Three paragraphs of waffle won't improve the answer. What helped was specific facts and a clear goal: a Marketplace ad, 5–6 lines."
  - text: "Claude just tried harder the second time"
    correct: false
    why: "Claude doesn't slack off or “try harder”. It works with what you give it. The first prompt gave it nothing to work with, so it wrote generic filler."
  - text: "The first answer is actually better: it sounds nicer"
    correct: false
    why: "It sounds smooth, but buyers won't learn the size, the price or the area. An ad like that gets questions, not buyers. A good answer gets the job done, not just sounds nice."
hint: "Find details in the second answer that aren't in the first. Where did Claude get them from?"
:::

::: step explain
How do you check a prompt before sending it? Use the **colleague test**.

Imagine handing your prompt to a colleague who knows nothing about it. Could they get started right away, or would they start asking: “Which bike? Where are you selling it? How much?”

If your colleague would be confused, so will Claude. Add the answers to their questions to your prompt.
:::

::: exercise match_pairs
id: "b2.1.e2"
type: "match_pairs"
question: "Run the colleague test. Match each vague prompt with the first question a colleague would ask."
pairs:
  - left: "“Make a presentation for Monday”"
    right: "“What's it about, and who are you presenting it to?”"
    why: "Without a topic and an audience, neither the content nor the level is clear. A presentation for the CEO and one for schoolkids are two different presentations."
  - left: "“Plan a week of meals”"
    right: "“For how many people, and is there anything you don't eat?”"
    why: "A meal plan for one student and one for a family with a child with allergies are completely different. Without those facts you'll get a plan for “the average person”."
  - left: "“Find a gift for a colleague”"
    right: "“What are they into, and what's your budget?”"
    why: "Interests and budget instantly rule out hundreds of unsuitable ideas. Without them, Claude will suggest a mug and a notebook."
  - left: "“Translate this email”"
    right: "“Into which language, and who's it for: you, or a business partner?”"
    why: "Translating “to understand it” and “to send it” are different tasks: in the second case, tone and polite phrasing matter."
hint: "Put yourself in your colleague's shoes. What information would you absolutely need before getting started?"
:::

::: exercise find_error
id: "b2.1.e3"
type: "find_error"
question: "Natalie writes to Claude. Tap every fragment where a colleague with no context would get stuck."
prompt: "Write a decent email to my boss about that situation so he gets it."
segments:
  - text: "Write "
    weak: false
    why: "It's clear what needs doing: writing. That's a good start."
  - text: "a decent "
    weak: true
    why: "Everyone's idea of “decent” is different. Polite? Short? Formal? Better to say it directly: “short and polite, no excuses”."
  - text: "email to my boss "
    weak: false
    why: "It's clear it's an email and who it's for. You could add how formal you are with each other, but the essentials are there."
  - text: "about that situation "
    weak: true
    why: "This is the biggest gap. Natalie knows what situation she means, Claude doesn't. It needs facts: what happened, when and what's already been done."
  - text: "so he gets it."
    weak: true
    why: "What exactly should the boss understand, and what should he do after reading? Approve a new deadline? Just hear the news? Name the goal of the email."
hint: "Read each piece as someone meeting Natalie for the first time. At which words would you ask: “Meaning what? Which one exactly?”"
:::

::: exercise order_steps
id: "b2.1.e4"
type: "order_steps"
question: "Put the steps of the colleague test in order."
shuffle: true
items:
  - text: "Write the prompt however it comes out"
    why: "Draft first. It doesn't need to be perfect straight away: editing is easier than starting from scratch."
  - text: "Reread it as a colleague who knows nothing about the situation"
    why: "This is the heart of the test: for a minute, forget everything you know and keep only what's on the page."
  - text: "Write down the questions they'd ask"
    why: "“Which one? Who for? How much? By when?” Each question shows what information is missing."
  - text: "Add the answers to those questions to your prompt"
    why: "Now the prompt stands on its own: it contains everything you know."
  - text: "Send the prompt to Claude"
    why: "Only send it after checking. That way you'll more often get the answer you need first time, without follow-up corrections."
hint: "You need a draft first, and sending comes last. In between, you first look for what's missing, then add it."
:::

::: step summary
**Claude is a smart new colleague: it knows a lot, but it doesn't know your situation.**

Before sending, run the colleague test: if someone with no context would start asking questions, add the answers to your prompt.

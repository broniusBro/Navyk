::: step hook
Sophie asks Claude to look over her CV:

```prompt
Have a look at my CV: [CV text]
```

```claude
Your CV looks good! Your experience is clearly described and your skills are listed. You could polish the layout a little and add a few words about yourself. Good luck with the job hunt!
```

Nice to hear, but what exactly should she fix? What if she asked Claude to look at the CV through a recruiter's eyes?
:::

::: step explain
A *role* is who Claude will be in the conversation. Just like in real life: you can ask a friend about your bad back, or you can ask a physio. The friend will sympathise; the specialist will ask precise questions and give you exercises.

The people who make Claude say that even one sentence about a role noticeably changes the tone and focus of the answer.
:::

::: exercise compare_answers
id: "b2.5.e1"
type: "compare_answers"
variant: "why_better"
question: "Sophie added a role. Why is the second answer more useful?"
cards:
  - id: "A"
    label: "No role"
    prompt: "Have a look at my CV: [CV text]"
    answer: |
      Your CV looks good! Your experience is clearly described and your skills are listed. You could polish the layout a little and add a few words about yourself. Good luck with the job hunt!
  - id: "B"
    label: "With a role"
    prompt: "You're an experienced IT recruiter who goes through 200 CVs a day. Look at my CV for a junior QA tester job in Vilnius and tell me what would make you stop: [CV text]"
    answer: |
      In the first 10 seconds, three things would trip me up:
      1. There's no “QA” at the top — I won't immediately see what role you're going for.
      2. “Familiar with testing” is too vague. Say what you did: “found and documented 40 bugs in a training project, using Jira”.
      3. Your courses are at the end. For a junior role they're the main thing, so move them above your retail experience.
      A plus: B2 English is a real strength in Vilnius, so put it near the top.
options:
  - text: "The role told Claude what angle to look from and what to treat as important"
    correct: true
    why: "Yes. “A recruiter who sees 200 CVs a day” judges differently: what stands out in 10 seconds, what gets in the way, what makes a CV stand out. Hence specific points instead of general praise."
  - text: "Claude believed it was a real recruiter and learned something new"
    correct: false
    why: "A role doesn't give Claude new knowledge. It directs attention and tone: which of its knowledge to apply and how to speak."
  - text: "The role is the main thing in a prompt; the rest matters less"
    correct: false
    why: "Look: the second prompt also has the job, the city and the question “what would make you stop”. A role works together with context, not instead of it."
  - text: "The first answer is better: it's kinder"
    correct: false
    why: "Kindness is nice, but Sophie needs a job. An answer that gives her nothing to fix won't help her get through screening."
hint: "Ask yourself: why does a recruiter look at a CV differently from a friend?"
:::

::: exercise match_pairs
id: "b2.5.e2"
type: "match_pairs"
question: "Match each task with the role that suits it best."
pairs:
  - left: "Check an email to your landlord in German before sending it"
    right: "“You're a native German speaker who helps foreigners with formal letters”"
    why: "This role will catch not just mistakes but also phrases that don't belong in a formal letter."
  - left: "Explain to a 10-year-old why rainbows happen"
    right: "“You're a primary school teacher who explains things through simple experiments”"
    why: "The role sets the level and approach: simple words, an experiment with a glass of water, no formulas."
  - left: "Find the weak spots in a café business plan"
    right: "“You're a demanding investor who has seen hundreds of cafés fail”"
    why: "An investor looks for risks rather than praising. That's exactly what the plan needs to survive scrutiny."
  - left: "Plan a week of meals for a family"
    right: "“You're a nutritionist who cooks with ordinary supermarket ingredients”"
    why: "The role adds both nutrition knowledge and practicality: no hard-to-find ingredients."
hint: "Think about which specialist you'd go to with this task in real life."
:::

::: step explain
How to write a role:

- at the start of the prompt, in one sentence: “You're…”;
- with a detail that sets the angle: not just “a lawyer” but “a lawyer who explains contracts in plain language”;
- then everything you already know how to do: the task, the “why”, the format, the steps.
:::

::: exercise single_choice
id: "b2.5.e3"
type: "single_choice"
question: "Which role gives Claude the most clues about how to answer?"
options:
  - text: "“You're a running coach who gets beginners to their first 5K without injuries”"
    correct: true
    why: "It has a profession (coach), who it's for (beginners) and what matters (no injuries). Claude immediately understands both the level and the priorities."
  - text: "“You're an expert”"
    correct: false
    why: "An expert in what, and for whom? A role like this changes almost nothing: Claude already tries to answer knowledgeably."
  - text: "“You're the best coach in the world, a sporting genius”"
    correct: false
    why: "Big words don't give focus. Who the coach works with and what they care about matters more than how “great” they are."
  - text: "“You're not a robot, you're a human”"
    correct: false
    why: "That's not a role, it's an attempt to change Claude. It says nothing about a profession or the task."
hint: "A good role answers the questions “who are they?” and “what do they look at?”."
:::

::: exercise single_choice
id: "b2.5.e4"
type: "single_choice"
question: "Remember the lesson on the “why”. What else should be added to the prompt “You're an experienced HR manager. Check my cover letter”?"
options:
  - text: "Which job the letter is for and why you want that job in particular"
    correct: true
    why: "The role sets the angle; the context provides the material. Without the job, even the best HR manager can't say whether the letter fits."
  - text: "Another role: “and you're also a writer”"
    correct: false
    why: "Two roles often blur the focus. One good role is enough, but there's no context here yet."
  - text: "The line “This is really important!!!”"
    correct: false
    why: "Emotion gives no facts. Better to explain why it matters: “it's my dream job, and the interview is in a week”."
hint: "The prompt already has a role. What's missing, based on the earlier lessons?"
:::

::: step explain
Now it's your turn. Give Claude a role and ask it to check your CV.

You don't need to attach a CV: write the prompt and put [CV] where the CV text would go. Don't forget the job and what kind of feedback you need. Navyk will send your prompt for feedback.
:::

::: exercise write_prompt
id: "b2.5.e5"
type: "write_prompt"
question: "Write a prompt that gives Claude a role and asks it to check your CV. Instead of the CV itself, write [CV]."
placeholder: "You're… Check my CV for a … job"
ideas:
  - "Who'll be looking: a recruiter, a team lead?"
  - "What job, and where?"
  - "What experience do you have?"
  - "What exactly should be checked?"
  - "What form should the feedback take?"
min_chars: 50
hint: "Start with “You're…” and one detail about what this person looks at. Then the job and your situation, then what the feedback should look like. Don't put your phone number, address or other personal details in the prompt."
review:
  task: "The learner gives Claude a role and asks it to check a CV (the CV text is replaced with [CV], which is fine). The lesson is about roles (P7): a one-sentence role that specifies the angle. Context (job, experience) and feedback format are revisited. Don't mark down for the missing CV itself. Assess the role under R1 (is the task clear, and from what position to look at it)."
  criteria: ["R1", "R2", "R3"]
  reference_prompt: |
    You're an HR manager at an IT company in Warsaw who hires junior staff and screens CVs in the first 30 seconds. Check my CV for a junior data analyst job. I'm switching from accounting, have 4 years' experience, and have completed a course in SQL and Power BI. Name the 3 main things that would make you put my CV aside, and for each one suggest how to rewrite the line. Keep the answer short, as a list.
    [CV]
  show_reference: true
  fallback_feedback:
    - "Is there a one-sentence role at the start: “You're…”?"
    - "Does it specify what this person looks at, e.g. “screens CVs in 30 seconds”?"
    - "Are the job and your experience given?"
    - "Does it say what feedback you need: how many points, with example fixes?"
    - "Is the prompt free of your phone number, address and other personal details?"
:::

::: step summary
**One sentence about a role sets the angle: “You're [who], who [what they look at]”.**

A role doesn't replace context, format and steps; it works together with them.

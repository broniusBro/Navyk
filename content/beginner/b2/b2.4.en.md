::: step hook
Andrew is moving from Cork to Dublin and writes to Claude in one long paragraph:

```prompt
I'm moving to Dublin in March, help, I need to find a flat and work out the money and some paperwork or something and I've already got a job, oh, and get the cat there.
```

```claude
Congratulations on the move! Dublin is a great city. Daft.ie is the easiest place to look for a flat, and it's best to start 3–4 weeks ahead. For the cat you'll need a carrier and possibly a vet check. Good luck in your new home!
```

Not a word about the budget or paperwork. Why?
:::

::: step explain
When there are lots of tasks all mixed together, Claude may latch onto one or two and lose the rest. Like a shopping list written on one line: you're bound to forget something.

The people who make Claude recommend this: if *order* or *completeness* matters, give the steps as a numbered list. Each task on its own line.
:::

::: exercise compare_answers
id: "b2.4.e1"
type: "compare_answers"
variant: "why_better"
question: "Andrew split his prompt into steps. Why is the second answer more complete?"
cards:
  - id: "A"
    label: "Before"
    prompt: "I'm moving to Dublin in March, help, I need to find a flat and work out the money and some paperwork or something and I've already got a job, oh, and get the cat there."
    answer: |
      Congratulations on the move! Daft.ie is the easiest place to look for a flat, and it's best to start 3–4 weeks ahead. For the cat you'll need a carrier and possibly a vet check. Good luck in your new home!
  - id: "B"
    label: "After"
    prompt: |
      In March I'm moving from Cork to Dublin: I've got a job there. I'm going on my own with my cat. Help me get ready step by step:
      1. Work out a rough budget for the first month.
      2. Tell me how and when to look for a flat.
      3. List the paperwork I need to sort out or update.
      4. Explain how to take the cat on the train.
    answer: |
      1. Budget. Allow for the first month's rent, a deposit (usually another month) and possibly an agency fee, plus travel, food until your first payday and a buffer for surprises. If you tell me which area you'd like, I can be more precise.
      2. Flat. Start looking 3–4 weeks before the move…
      3. Paperwork. Change of address, bank, GP…
      4. Cat. Carrier, rules for travelling with pets…
options:
  - text: "Each task became a separate point, and Claude went through all of them in order"
    correct: true
    why: "Yes. In one long paragraph, the budget and paperwork got “lost”. In a numbered list they can't be missed, and the answer follows the same structure, which makes it easy to work through."
  - text: "Claude answers better when there are numbers in the prompt"
    correct: false
    why: "It's not about numbers as such. Here the numbers are step numbers. They show Claude how many tasks there are and in what order to tackle them."
  - text: "Andrew was just more polite in the second prompt"
    correct: false
    why: "Politeness is nice, but it's not why the answer is more complete. What helped was having each task separate and in order."
  - text: "Claude just got lucky the second time"
    correct: false
    why: "Luck has nothing to do with it. When tasks are numbered, Claude sees all of them. It works reliably."
hint: "Compare how many of Andrew's tasks appear in the first answer and how many in the second."
:::

::: step explain
How to write steps:

- one step, one action with a verb: “work out”, “list”, “compare”;
- the order of real life: first whatever the next steps depend on;
- context and the “why” still go before the steps, as in earlier lessons.

You don't always need steps. For a simple question, one sentence is enough.
:::

::: exercise order_steps
id: "b2.4.e2"
type: "order_steps"
question: "Irene asks Claude to help organise her daughter's birthday party. Put the steps of her prompt in a logical order."
shuffle: true
items:
  - text: "Suggest 3 party ideas for an 8-year-old girl who loves drawing"
    why: "The idea comes first: everything else depends on it, from the venue to the shopping."
  - text: "For the chosen idea, plan a 2-hour programme"
    why: "The programme is built around a specific idea. Without the idea, you can't plan it."
  - text: "Make a shopping list for 10 children"
    why: "What to buy only becomes clear once the idea and programme are set: paints, paper, cake."
  - text: "Work out a rough budget from that list"
    why: "You can only work out the cost once you have the list. That's why this step comes after the shopping list."
  - text: "Write a short invitation for the parents' group chat"
    why: "The invitation is written once everything is decided: time, format, what to bring."
hint: "For each step, ask yourself: which other step does it depend on? That step should come earlier."
:::

::: exercise build_prompt
id: "b2.4.e3"
type: "build_prompt"
question: "Build the prompt for Ben, who is preparing for his driving theory test. Leave out what you don't need."
blocks:
  - id: "b1"
    text: "Help me prepare for my driving theory test in 3 weeks."
    role: "goal"
    why: "Goal and deadline straight away: Claude understands that a plan is needed, not a talk about the rules."
  - id: "b2"
    text: "I work until 6 pm, so I can only study for an hour in the evening and 3 hours on Saturday."
    role: "context"
    why: "Context and the “why”: work leaves little time. The plan will be realistic."
  - id: "b3"
    text: "1. Split the topics over 3 weeks. 2. For each day, give one topic and 10 minutes of revising earlier ones. 3. At the end of each week, a practice test."
    role: "format"
    why: "These are the steps Claude will use to build the plan. The numbers make sure revision and tests don't get lost."
  - id: "b4"
    text: "Exams are so stressful, aren't they?"
    role: "noise"
    why: "This isn't a task or a fact about you. Claude will respond with sympathy and waste part of the answer."
  - id: "b5"
    text: "Any order is fine, whatever you like."
    role: "noise"
    why: "This contradicts the steps. If order matters, don't cancel it with one sentence."
target_order: ["b1", "b2", "b3"]
hint: "Goal, then your situation, then the steps. Look for blocks that get in the way of that logic."
:::

::: exercise find_error
id: "b2.4.e4"
type: "find_error"
question: "This prompt has steps, but not everything's right. Tap the weak fragments."
prompt: "Help me write a CV for a sales assistant job. 1. Write the CV. 2. And all the other stuff I need. 3. Don't make it boring."
segments:
  - text: "Help me write a CV for a sales assistant job. "
    weak: false
    why: "The goal is clear. You could add where the job is and what experience you have, but it's a decent start."
  - text: "1. Write the CV. "
    weak: false
    why: "The step repeats the goal, but on its own it's clear: one action with a verb."
  - text: "2. And all the other stuff I need. "
    weak: true
    why: "That's not a step, it's a riddle. What exactly: a cover letter? Interview tips? Name the action."
  - text: "3. Don't make it boring."
    weak: true
    why: "A ban instead of a description, as in the last lesson. Better: “Keep it lively, with one concrete achievement for each job”."
hint: "A good step is one clear action with a verb. Remember the last lesson too: it's better to replace a “don't…” with a description."
:::

::: step explain
Now it's your turn. Ask Claude to put together a step-by-step plan for moving to another city: budget, housing, paperwork.

Make up the city and situation. Don't forget the context: who's moving, when and why. Navyk will send your prompt for feedback.
:::

::: step summary
**Complex task? Numbered steps, one action each.**

Situation and “why” first, then the steps in order. That way Claude won't miss anything, and you'll get an answer that's easy to work through.

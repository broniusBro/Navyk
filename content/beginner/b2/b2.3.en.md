::: step hook
Dan asks Claude about taxes as a freelancer:

```prompt
Explain how to pay tax as a freelancer in Ireland. No waffle and no lists!
```

```claude
Briefly and without lists: the main thing is to sort out how you're set up and file your returns on time, while bearing in mind… that said, there are a few key points: firstly, registration; secondly, choosing a structure; thirdly…
```

No list, but the answer is still a muddle. What went wrong?
:::

::: step explain
Dan said what he *doesn't* want. But he didn't say what he does want.

It's like telling a taxi driver: “Anywhere but the centre.” There are still hundreds of options. Better to give the address.

Same with answers: describe what the answer should be like. That's exactly what the people who make Claude recommend: say what to do, not what not to do.
:::

::: exercise single_choice
id: "b2.3.e1"
type: "single_choice"
question: "Which wording describes the answer you want rather than a ban?"
options:
  - text: "“Explain it in 3–4 connected paragraphs, as if talking to a friend who knows nothing about tax”"
    correct: true
    why: "It has everything needed: length (3–4 paragraphs), structure (flowing text), tone (like to a friend) and level (for a beginner). Claude gets an “address”, not a list of places not to go."
  - text: "“No waffle, no lists, no jargon”"
    correct: false
    why: "Three bans and no description. Claude has to guess what's left after all the “no”s. Better to say: “short, in paragraphs, in plain words”."
  - text: "“Don't make it too long and don't make it too short”"
    correct: false
    why: "Two bans that set nothing. Name the length directly: “5–7 sentences” or “one phone screen”."
  - text: "“Just not like a textbook”"
    correct: false
    why: "It's clear what to avoid, but not what to aim for. Like a conversation? Like a beginner's article? Like step-by-step instructions? Say it directly."
hint: "Look for the option that lets you picture the finished answer before Claude even writes it."
:::

::: step explain
What you can set in an answer:

- *length*: “5–7 sentences”, “one phone screen”;
- *structure*: “in paragraphs”, “as a table: item, price, where to buy”;
- *tone*: “friendly”, “formal”;
- *language*: “in German”, “in English, B1 level”.

One more tip: Claude often writes the way your prompt is written. Want a simple answer? Write simply.
:::

::: exercise find_error
id: "b2.3.e2"
type: "find_error"
question: "Tap every fragment where there's a ban or an empty word instead of a description of the answer."
prompt: "Write a reply to a customer who's complaining that their delivery is 5 days late. Don't make it long, don't be too formal, and make it decent."
segments:
  - text: "Write a reply to a customer "
    weak: false
    why: "The task is clear: a reply to a customer. Good start."
  - text: "who's complaining that their delivery is 5 days late. "
    weak: false
    why: "This is context: what happened and how bad it is. With it, Claude can apologise for the actual problem."
  - text: "Don't make it long, "
    weak: true
    why: "A ban instead of a measure. How long is that: 2 sentences or 10? Better: “3–4 sentences”."
  - text: "don't be too formal, "
    weak: true
    why: "It's clear what to avoid, but not what tone is needed. Better: “warm and human, but still polite”."
  - text: "and make it decent."
    weak: true
    why: "“Decent” says nothing. Describe what a good reply is: for example, “apologise, give the new delivery date and offer 10% off their next order”."
hint: "Check each piece: can you picture the finished answer from it? If it's just a “don't” or a vague word, tap it."
:::

::: exercise fill_gap
id: "b2.3.e3"
type: "fill_gap"
question: "Sophie asks Claude to help with an ad to rent out her flat. Choose the wording that describes the answer she wants."
text: "Write an ad to rent out a one-bedroom flat in Ghent. Length: ___. Tone: ___. Structure: ___."
gaps:
  - options:
      - text: "6–8 lines, so it fits in the listing preview"
        correct: true
        why: "A specific length and a reason. Claude knows how much to write and why."
      - text: "not too long"
        correct: false
        why: "This is a ban with no measure. Everyone's idea of “not too” is different."
      - text: "whatever works"
        correct: false
        why: "Then the length will be random. Better to give the number of lines."
  - options:
      - text: "calm and businesslike, no exclamation marks"
        correct: false
        why: "“Calm and businesslike” is a good description, but “no exclamation marks” is an unnecessary ban. Describing the tone you want is enough."
      - text: "calm and businesslike, like a landlady who values tidy tenants"
        correct: true
        why: "The tone is described in words and with an image. Claude knows how to sound without any bans."
      - text: "not pushy"
        correct: false
        why: "A ban instead of a description. So what should the tone be: businesslike, warm, reserved?"
  - options:
      - text: "price and area first, then a description of the flat, then terms and contact at the end"
        correct: true
        why: "The structure is described step by step. What matters most to a tenant (price and area) is visible straight away."
      - text: "no unnecessary information"
        correct: false
        why: "Claude would have to guess what's unnecessary. Better to list what's needed and in what order."
      - text: "any, you know best"
        correct: false
        why: "Claude will write something sensible, but not necessarily what works for Sophie. If the structure matters, describe it."
hint: "Choose options that say what the answer should be like, not what it shouldn't be."
:::

::: exercise improve_prompt
id: "b2.3.e5"
type: "improve_prompt"
question: "Rewrite the prompt so it describes the answer you want, not bans."
weak_prompt: "Explain taxes, no waffle and no lists"
placeholder: "Explain… I'd like the answer to be: …"
ideas:
  - "Which taxes, and in which country?"
  - "Who are you: freelancer, student, business owner?"
  - "How long should the answer be?"
  - "Paragraphs, steps or a table?"
  - "What tone: like a friend or like a lawyer?"
min_chars: 40
hint: "Swap every “no…” for “how it should be”. “No waffle” → how many sentences? “No lists” → connected paragraphs? Don't forget to say who you are and why you need this."
review:
  task: "The learner is rewriting “Explain taxes, no waffle and no lists”. The lesson is about format (P3): length, structure, tone and language should be described as the desired result, not as bans. Point out any bans left without a description. Context (who the learner is, which country) repeats earlier lessons: it's good if it's there."
  criteria: ["R1", "R2", "R3"]
  reference_prompt: |
    Explain how to pay tax as a freelance designer based in Cork who gets paid by clients abroad. I'm a complete beginner and want to understand the big picture before I see an accountant. Answer in 4–5 connected paragraphs in plain language, like you're explaining it to a friend over coffee: what I need to register, roughly how much I'll pay, and when and where to file returns. End with one sentence on what I should double-check with an accountant.
  show_reference: true
  fallback_feedback:
    - "Has every “no…” been replaced with a description of what you want?"
    - "Is the length set: number of sentences, paragraphs or lines?"
    - "Is the structure given: paragraphs, steps, a table?"
    - "Is the tone described, e.g. “like to a friend”, “formal”?"
    - "Is it clear who you are and why you need this answer?"
:::

::: step summary
**Describe what the answer should be like: length, structure, tone, language.**

Instead of “no waffle”, write “5 sentences”. Instead of “not formal”, write “like to a friend”. Give the address, not the places not to go.

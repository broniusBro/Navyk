::: step hook
Maria is choosing a laptop and writes to Claude:

```prompt
Recommend a laptop under €700
```

```claude
There are a few good directions under €700:
1. A gaming laptop with a dedicated graphics card — for games and heavy software.
2. A light ultrabook — for studying and travel.
3. An all-rounder with a big screen — for home and office.
Tell me what you need it for, and I can be more specific.
```

Claude wasn't wrong, but it didn't help either. What was it missing?
:::

::: step explain
In the last lesson you added facts to your prompt. Now let's add one more thing: *why* you need it.

It's like seeing a doctor. “I have a headache” isn't much to go on. “It hurts by evening, after eight hours at the screen” — and the doctor is already thinking in the right direction.

The goal and circumstances of a task are called *context*.
:::

::: exercise compare_answers
id: "b2.2.e1"
type: "compare_answers"
variant: "why_better"
question: "Maria explained why she needs a laptop. Why is the second answer more useful?"
cards:
  - id: "A"
    label: "Before"
    prompt: "Recommend a laptop under €700"
    answer: |
      There are a few good directions under €700: a gaming laptop, a light ultrabook or an all-rounder with a big screen. Tell me what you need it for, and I can be more specific.
  - id: "B"
    label: "After"
    prompt: "Tell me what to look for when choosing a laptop. I'm studying design in Lyon, I work in Figma and a bit in Photoshop. I carry my laptop to classes every day, so weight and all-day battery matter. My budget is strictly €700: it's my savings, and there won't be any more."
    answer: |
      For Figma, light Photoshop work and carrying it every day, look for:
      — at least 16 GB of RAM: Figma with large files eats it up first;
      — an IPS or OLED screen with accurate colour: you're a designer, so colours need to be true;
      — under 1.5 kg and at least 8 hours of real-world battery life;
      — no need for a powerful graphics card: it would eat into your budget, and it doesn't matter for your work.
      That way you'll stay within €700 and won't spend money on things you don't need.
options:
  - text: "Claude learned what the laptop is for and why weight and budget matter, so it could rule out the extras"
    correct: true
    why: "Yes. Knowing the goal (design in Figma, carried to classes), Claude worked out what matters (RAM, screen, weight) and what she can skip (a powerful graphics card). The reason “it's my savings” explained why the budget can't be stretched."
  - text: "The second prompt has the magic words “tell me what to look for”"
    correct: false
    why: "The wording helped a little, but that's not the main thing. Without mentioning Figma and daily classes, Claude would have produced the same generic list of directions."
  - text: "The second answer is better because it has more points"
    correct: false
    why: "The number of points has nothing to do with it. What matters is that each point is tied to Maria's needs. A long but generic list would have helped as little as the first one."
  - text: "The first answer is worse because Claude asked a question instead of answering"
    correct: false
    why: "The question was honest: Claude realised it was missing context. That's a sign the prompt failed the “colleague test” from the last lesson. It's better to answer those questions in the prompt straight away."
hint: "Look at the second answer: how did Claude know Maria doesn't need a graphics card?"
:::

::: step explain
The people who make Claude give this example. The rule “Never use ellipses” works worse than a rule with a reason:

```prompt
This text will be read aloud by a text-to-speech engine. It can't pronounce ellipses, so don't use them.
```

Once it knows the reason, Claude will work out on its own to avoid other things the engine can't read.
:::

::: exercise single_choice
id: "b2.2.e2"
type: "single_choice"
question: "Tim is writing the script for an audio guide to Lisbon. Which rule will Claude understand best?"
options:
  - text: "“Tourists will listen to this on headphones while walking, so use short sentences, no tables and no abbreviations”"
    correct: true
    why: "There's a reason here: the text is heard, not read, and heard on the move. Claude will also pick up what Tim didn't say outright, for example that long numbers and brackets are hard to follow by ear."
  - text: "“NEVER USE TABLES!!!”"
    correct: false
    why: "Shouting doesn't explain the reason. Claude will drop the tables but may keep abbreviations, brackets and long sentences, which are just as awkward to listen to."
  - text: "“Write well and correctly”"
    correct: false
    why: "“Well” means different things for an article and an audio guide. Without saying the text will be listened to, Claude will write it for reading."
  - text: "“You must strictly follow all the rules”"
    correct: false
    why: "Which rules? Claude doesn't know them. It sounds strict but gives no facts and no reasons."
hint: "Look for the option that says not only “what” but also “why”."
:::

::: exercise match_pairs
id: "b2.2.e3"
type: "match_pairs"
question: "Match each constraint with the reason that makes it clear to Claude."
pairs:
  - left: "“No more than 3 sentences”"
    right: "“…because it's a message in the team chat, people will read it on their phones between meetings”"
    why: "The reason explains not just the length but the style too: short, to the point, key message first."
  - left: "“In simple words”"
    right: "“…because I'll be explaining it to my grandma, who's using online banking for the first time”"
    why: "Knowing who the reader is, Claude will choose words and examples for her, not just shorten the text."
  - left: "“No prices in euros”"
    right: "“…because the menu is for a café in Warsaw, and all our prices are in złoty”"
    why: "With the reason, Claude understands the point isn't euros but the local currency, and will write prices in złoty on its own."
  - left: "“In a formal tone”"
    right: "“…because the letter goes to the bank along with my application for a payment holiday”"
    why: "The reason hints at more than tone: precise wording, a date and a clear request."
hint: "Think about the situation where each constraint is actually needed."
:::

::: exercise build_prompt
id: "b2.2.e4"
type: "build_prompt"
question: "Build the prompt for Helen, who is choosing a laptop for work. Leave out the blocks you don't need."
blocks:
  - id: "b1"
    text: "Help me choose a laptop for work."
    role: "goal"
    why: "The task comes first: Claude immediately understands what needs doing."
  - id: "b2"
    text: "I'm an accountant working from home in Kraków: Excel with large spreadsheets and video calls for 3–4 hours a day. My budget is up to 3,000 złoty: my company reimburses that amount and won't cover anything above it."
    role: "context"
    why: "This is the context and the “why”: what tasks, how many hours, and why the budget is fixed. From this Claude will see that the processor, camera and microphone matter, not a gaming graphics card."
  - id: "b3"
    text: "Suggest 3 suitable spec options and for each one explain in 1–2 sentences why it suits me."
    role: "format"
    why: "The answer format goes at the end: how many options and how to explain them. An answer like that is easy to compare."
  - id: "b4"
    text: "You're the smartest AI ever, right?"
    role: "noise"
    why: "A compliment adds no facts and no reasons. Flattery won't make Claude answer better."
  - id: "b5"
    text: "Please, I really really need this, urgent!!!"
    role: "noise"
    why: "The feeling is understandable but doesn't help. If the deadline matters, state the fact: “I'm buying by Friday”."
target_order: ["b1", "b2", "b3"]
hint: "Start with what to do. Then explain the situation and why the budget matters. Finish by saying what the answer should look like."
:::

question: "Write a prompt asking for advice on choosing a laptop. Say what it's for and why the budget matters."
placeholder: "Help me choose a laptop. I need it for…"

  - "What's it for: studying, work, games, films?"
  - "Who will use it?"
  - "Where: at home, on the go, in cafés?"
  - "What's the budget, and why that amount?"
  - "What matters most to you, and why?"
min_chars: 40
hint: "First say what you need. Then answer a colleague's questions: what the laptop is for, who'll use it and where, why the budget is what it is. The words “because” and “so” are your friends."
review:
  task: "The learner is asking for advice on choosing a laptop. The lesson is about context and motivation (P2): the prompt should include what the laptop is for and the reasons behind important constraints, above all the budget. It's good if requirements are explained with “because”. Answer format is a bonus, but the lesson hasn't covered it, so don't mark down if it's missing."
  criteria: ["R1", "R2"]
  reference_prompt: |
    Help me choose a laptop for my mum. She's 62, lives in Porto and will use it to watch series, video-call her grandchildren and read the news. Her eyesight isn't great any more, so a big screen of at least 15 inches and a good camera for calls matter. The budget is up to €500: it's a joint birthday present from the family, and we can't raise more. Suggest 2–3 spec options and briefly explain why each one suits her.
  show_reference: true
  fallback_feedback:
    - "Is it clear what the laptop is for and who will use it?"
    - "Did you explain why the budget matters: where it comes from and whether it can be stretched?"
    - "Is there at least one “because” or “so” next to an important requirement?"
    - "Did you mention how it'll be used: at home, on the go, how many hours a day?"
    - "Colleague test: could a shop assistant help you with this prompt without asking anything?"
:::

::: step summary
**Tell Claude not just “what” but also “why”.**

Explain the reasons behind constraints with “because”. Once Claude knows the goal, it will rule out what you don't need and focus on what matters.

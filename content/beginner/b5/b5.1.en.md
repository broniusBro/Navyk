::: step hook
Marta lives in Wrocław and starts almost every new chat with Claude the same way:

```prompt
Reply in English, keep it short and skip the jargon, I'm not a tech person. Now my question: how do I return a parcel with InPost?
```

That first sentence gets repeated day after day. She could say it once, and Claude would take it into account in every chat. Here's how.
:::

::: step explain
*Instructions for Claude* are a standing note about you. Claude takes it into account in every conversation.

It's like a note for the babysitter on the fridge: “Nut allergy, bedtime 9 pm.” You write it once instead of repeating it every evening.

Where to find it: click your initials in the bottom left, then {{data.ui_paths.settings_instructions}}.
:::

::: step explain
What to put there:

- which language to reply in;
- how detailed and in what shape;
- who you are and what you do;
- words and topics that come up for you a lot.

Say *what to do*: “reply in short paragraphs” instead of “don't write walls of text”. And add *why*: “I read on my phone on the go”. That way Claude knows what to do in new situations too.
:::

::: exercise single_choice
id: "b5.1.e1"
type: "single_choice"
question: "Which of these should Marta put in Instructions for Claude?"
options:
  - text: "“Reply in English, in plain words: I'm not a tech person and I usually read on my phone”"
    correct: true
    why: "Marta needs this in every chat: about parcels, recipes or work. Standing preferences like this are exactly what instructions for every chat are for. And the “why” (reading on a phone) helps Claude pick the right length."
  - text: "“Help me write a complaint to InPost: my parcel never arrived”"
    correct: false
    why: "This is a one-off task. It belongs in a regular chat. Put it in the instructions and Claude will keep thinking about the complaint even when you're chatting about a soup recipe."
  - text: "“In this project you're helping me prepare for my B1 Polish exam”"
    correct: false
    why: "This rule is for one topic only, Polish. It belongs in the instructions of a “Polish” project, so it doesn't get in the way of other chats."
  - text: "“My bank password, so you remember it”"
    correct: false
    why: "Passwords don't go in instructions, chats or projects. It's like leaving your house keys in the letterbox. Use a password manager for that."
hint: "Look for something Marta will need in any conversation, not in one task or one topic."
:::

::: step explain
Compare the two levels, like house rules:

- *Instructions for Claude* are rules for the whole home: “shoes off at the door”. They apply in every chat.
- *Project instructions* are rules for one room: “quiet in the kids' room after nine”. They apply only to chats inside that project.

Language and reply style go in your profile. A role and tasks for one topic go in a project.
:::

::: exercise match_pairs
id: "b5.1.e2"
type: "match_pairs"
question: "Dan from Dublin is sorting out his preferences. Match each one with the place it belongs."
pairs:
  - left: "“I'm in Ireland: show amounts in euros and dates as DD/MM/YYYY”"
    right: "Instructions for Claude"
    why: "Dan needs euros and that date format in almost every conversation. It's a general preference, so it belongs in the settings for every chat."
  - left: "“You're helping me keep the books for my café: answer like a small-business accountant”"
    right: "Instructions for the “Café” project"
    why: "The accountant role is only needed in conversations about the café. In a chat about a holiday or a gift for his mum it would get in the way, so it goes in the project."
  - left: "“Make this post a third shorter”"
    right: "A message in the current chat"
    why: "This is a request about a specific text, right now. No need to save it in settings: it does its job in this chat."
  - left: "“The login and password for the till”"
    right: "Nowhere: you don't give this to Claude"
    why: "Passwords and logins don't belong in instructions, projects or chats. If Claude needs context, “my till system is from such-and-such company” is enough."
hint: "Ask yourself about each preference: is it needed always, only for one topic, or only right now?"
:::

::: exercise find_error
id: "b5.1.e3"
type: "find_error"
question: "Dan has written his instructions for every chat. Tap every fragment that should be rewritten."
prompt: "I'm Dan, a logistics manager in Dublin. Reply properly. Don't write too much. My work email password: Sunny2024. Reply in British English and show amounts in euros."
segments:
  - text: "I'm Dan, a logistics manager in Dublin. "
    weak: false
    why: "Good: who he is and what he does. Claude can pick examples from logistics and keep his location in mind."
  - text: "Reply properly. "
    weak: true
    why: "Everyone has a different idea of “properly”. Better to say it directly: “in plain words, main conclusion first, then the details”."
  - text: "Don't write too much. "
    weak: true
    why: "This is a ban, not an instruction. Better to say what to do, and why: “reply in 3–5 sentences, I read between meetings”."
  - text: "My work email password: Sunny2024. "
    weak: true
    why: "A password never goes in instructions or a chat. Claude doesn't need it for any task. Delete this line and change the password if it's already ended up somewhere."
  - text: "Reply in British English and show amounts in euros."
    weak: false
    why: "A clear, useful everyday preference. This is exactly the kind of thing that goes in instructions for every chat."
hint: "Look for three things: a vague word, a ban with no instruction on what to do instead, and something you should never trust to any chat."
:::

::: step explain
Now it's your turn. Write the instructions for all your chats. A good set:

- the language for replies;
- length and shape;
- who you are and what you usually come to Claude for.

Navyk will send the text for feedback. Then you can paste it into {{data.ui_paths.settings_instructions}}.
:::

review:
  task: "The learner is writing Instructions for Claude, the general instructions that apply in all their chats. A good text covers: the reply language, preferred length and shape of replies, who the learner is and what tasks they usually bring, and why this works for them. Preferences are phrased as actions, not bans (P3). There should be no one-off tasks or single-topic rules: if there are, gently suggest moving them to a project or a regular chat. Passwords, ID numbers or an exact address are a blocking R10 error."
  criteria: ["R1", "R2", "R3", "R4"]
  reference_prompt: |
    Reply in English, even if I write in Polish or Ukrainian.
    I live in Wrocław, work as a receptionist at a dental clinic and I'm not very techy, so explain things in plain words and translate any terms straight away.
    I usually read replies on my phone, so keep it short: the main point first in 1–2 sentences, then the steps as a list.
    Most often I ask for help with emails to patients, Polish paperwork and planning my week.
    If you don't have enough information for a good answer, ask me one clarifying question.
  show_reference: true
  fallback_feedback:
    - "Does it say which language to reply in?"
    - "Does it say what length and shape of reply works for you?"
    - "Are there a couple of lines about you: what you do, what you usually come for?"
    - "Does it explain why this works for you, e.g. “I read on my phone”?"
    - "Does every rule sound like an action (“write…”) rather than a ban (“don't write…”)?"
    - "Is it free of one-off tasks and single-topic rules that belong in a chat or a project?"
    - "Is it free of passwords, ID numbers and your exact address?"
:::

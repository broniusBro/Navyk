::: step hook
Liam has moved from Dublin to Amsterdam. But Claude still recommends cafés in Dublin. He could dig through the settings, but there's a quicker way: just say it in the chat.

```prompt
Remember: I've moved from Dublin to Amsterdam. From now on, give me city tips for Amsterdam.
```

Today, three moves: remember, forget, find a past chat.
:::

::: step explain
You can talk to memory in plain words, like to a personal assistant: “Note this down”, “Correct that”, “Cross it out”.

- *Remember*: “Remember that…”
- *Update*: “Update your memory: now…”
- *Forget*: “Forget that…”

Be specific: which fact and what to do with it. The change kicks in from the *next* chat.
:::

::: exercise fill_gap
id: "b5.3.e1"
type: "fill_gap"
question: "Fill in the gaps in Liam's messages."
text: "To make Claude take the news into account in future chats: “___ that I work remotely now”. To remove something outdated: “___ about my office job on Baggot Street”. The new rules will apply ___."
gaps:
  - options:
      - text: "Remember"
        correct: true
        why: "A direct “remember” is the clearest signal that the fact should be saved to memory."
      - text: "Think about"
        correct: false
        why: "“Think about” asks Claude to reason about it now, not to save the fact for later. It might not note it down at all."
      - text: "Maybe"
        correct: false
        why: "A hesitant phrase doesn't say what to do. Better to be direct: “remember”."
  - options:
      - text: "Forget"
        correct: true
        why: "“Forget” plus a specific fact is a clear command to remove an entry. Even more precise is “update”: “I work from home now”."
      - text: "Ignore what I said"
        correct: false
        why: "That might only work in the current conversation. To get the entry out of memory, say it directly: “forget”."
      - text: "Save"
        correct: false
        why: "“Save” would do the opposite: reinforce the old note about the office."
  - options:
      - text: "from the next chat"
        correct: true
        why: "Yes. Memory changes apply to the next conversation. In the current chat, Claude can already see your message anyway."
      - text: "in a month"
        correct: false
        why: "No need to wait a month. New entries appear during the conversation and apply from the very next chat."
      - text: "only after restarting your computer"
        correct: false
        why: "Memory is stored in your account, not on your computer. Restarting makes no difference."
hint: "You need three things: a command to save, a command to remove, and when it all takes effect."
:::

::: step explain
Sometimes you don't want memory in a conversation at all. Say you're looking for a gift for your partner on a shared laptop.

Before your first message, click {{data.ui_paths.chat_plus_menu}} and turn off {{data.ui_paths.chat_memory_toggle}}. After the first message the toggle can't be changed: you'll need a new chat.

That chat stays in your history, just without memory.
:::

::: exercise match_pairs
id: "b5.3.e2"
type: "match_pairs"
question: "Match each situation with what Liam should do."
pairs:
  - left: "He's signed up for swimming on Tuesdays and wants Claude to factor it into plans"
    right: "Write: “Remember: I have swimming every Tuesday at 7 pm”"
    why: "A new, ongoing fact: save it to memory and Claude will factor it into future weekly plans."
  - left: "Claude thinks Liam has a cat, but he has a dog"
    right: "Write: “Update your memory: I don't have a cat, I have a dog, a dachshund called Biscuit”"
    why: "The entry is wrong, but the topic is useful. Correcting it is more precise than deleting it: Claude learns the right fact straight away."
  - left: "Doesn't want Claude to remember the money he owes a friend any more"
    right: "Write: “Forget everything about the money I owe Andy”"
    why: "A specific “forget” command with the fact named. Afterwards you can look in Settings > Memory to check the topic is gone."
  - left: "Wants to plan a surprise for his wife's birthday without it going into memory"
    right: "New chat, turn off Memory in the “+” menu before the first message"
    why: "That way the conversation won't use or add to memory, but it'll stay in your history. If you don't want it in your history either, there's incognito chat, covered in the next lesson."
hint: "For each situation, decide: add, correct, remove, or leave memory out of this conversation entirely."
:::

::: step explain
The third move: *searching past chats*. Don't retell the whole story, just point to it:

```prompt
Find our conversation about the bathroom renovation in March and let's pick up where we left off: which tiles did we choose?
```

Claude will find the chat and link to it. Search doesn't cross project boundaries. Available on: {{data.plans.chat_search_availability}}. You can turn it off in {{data.ui_paths.settings_memory}}.
:::

::: exercise find_error
id: "b5.3.e3"
type: "find_error"
question: "Liam wants to go back to a past conversation. Tap the fragments that leave Claude unsure which chat to look for and what to do."
prompt: "Remember we talked about something recently? Find that chat about it. And do it like there."
segments:
  - text: "Remember we talked about something recently? "
    weak: true
    why: "“Something” and “recently” won't help find the chat. Name the topic and roughly when: “about the bathroom renovation in March”."
  - text: "Find that chat "
    weak: false
    why: "Asking to find a past conversation is clear. This part is fine, it just needs to say which one."
  - text: "about it. "
    weak: true
    why: "Claude can't guess what “it” is. Keywords from the topic help the search: “tiles”, “bathroom”, “quote”."
  - text: "And do it like there."
    weak: true
    why: "It's unclear what to actually do. Continue the list? Update the quote? Say the task directly: “recalculate the quote with the new tile price”."
hint: "Run the colleague test: at which words would they ask “what exactly?”, “when?”, “do what?”"
:::

::: exercise single_choice
id: "b5.3.e4"
type: "single_choice"
question: "Liam discussed the wedding budget in his “Wedding” project. Now he's writing in a regular chat outside any project: “Find what we decided about the wedding budget”. What will most likely happen?"
options:
  - text: "Claude won't find that conversation: search doesn't cross project boundaries. He needs to ask inside the “Wedding” project"
    correct: true
    why: "Right. Search works either across chats outside projects or inside a single project. That's deliberate, so topics don't get mixed up. Open the project and ask there."
  - text: "Claude will find the conversation in any project, search sees everything"
    correct: false
    why: "No, each project is its own separate space. A chat outside projects can't see conversations inside a project, and vice versa."
  - text: "Claude will open an incognito chat and search there"
    correct: false
    why: "Incognito chats never show up in search at all. And they have nothing to do with projects."
hint: "Think back to the search screen: what did it say about project boundaries?"
:::

::: step summary
**You can talk to memory in plain words: “remember”, “update”, “forget”. Be specific: which fact and what to do with it.**

Point to a past chat by topic and time instead of retelling it. Need a conversation without memory? Turn off Memory in “+” before the first message.

Next up: incognito chat.

::: step hook
At the start of the month, Emma from Ghent told Claude about her bookkeeping course. Today she opens a new chat and asks for weekend ideas. The reply says:

```claude
Here are a few ideas. Since you're training as a bookkeeper at the moment, you could keep one quiet Saturday for revision and save Sunday for a walk in Citadelpark…
```

Emma was surprised: “How does it know that?” Let's find out where this is stored and how to see it.
:::

::: step explain
*Memory* is what Claude remembers about you from your conversations, so it doesn't have to ask again.

It's like your hairdresser's notebook: “Emma, layered cut, hates a short fringe.” Next time there's nothing to explain.

Claude adds to the notebook itself as you chat. Entries are sorted into *Topics*: work, studies, family, habits.
:::

::: step explain
You can open the notebook: {{data.ui_paths.settings_memory}}. There you'll find the {{data.ui_paths.memory_topics}} list.

- Click a topic to read it.
- The pencil icon lets you edit it.
- {{data.ui_paths.memory_delete}} removes the topic.

Your edits apply to all future chats. Memory availability by plan: {{data.plans.memory_availability}}.
:::

::: step explain
Three things worth knowing for peace of mind:

- Deleted a chat? The memories from it *stay*. You delete them separately in Topics.
- By default, Claude doesn't remember health, religion or politics. It never saves ID or bank account numbers.
- {{data.ui_paths.memory_pause}} pauses memory; {{data.ui_paths.memory_reset}} wipes it completely and for good.
:::

::: exercise match_pairs
id: "b5.2.e3"
type: "match_pairs"
question: "Emma has found various entries in memory. Match each situation with the right action."
pairs:
  - left: "It says “training as a bookkeeper”, but she's already finished the course"
    right: "Open the topic and fix it with the pencil icon"
    why: "The entry is almost right, just out of date. It's easier to change it to “works as a bookkeeper” than to delete it and wait for Claude to learn it again."
  - left: "A topic about her ex, whom she'd rather not be reminded of"
    right: "Delete the topic with the Delete button"
    why: "Emma doesn't need this entry any more. A deleted topic won't come up in future chats. The old chat itself can be deleted separately."
  - left: "Wants to stop adding to memory for a month but keep the old entries"
    right: "Pause memory"
    why: "Pausing keeps the entries in place, but Claude stops using them and doesn't make new ones. Conversations from the paused period won't make it into memory later either."
  - left: "Is giving her laptop to her sister and wants to erase everything Claude remembers about her"
    right: "Reset memory"
    why: "A reset deletes all memory, including project memory, and can't be undone. So it's a last resort, not a way to fix a single entry."
hint: "Think about scale: one entry or all of memory, fix or remove, for a while or for good."
:::

::: step summary
**Memory is notes about you, sorted by topic. You can see them in Settings > Memory, and you're in control.**

Read, edit, delete. Deleting a chat doesn't erase memory: check Topics.

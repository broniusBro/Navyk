::: step hook
Daniel from Dublin asks Claude to help with a complaint to his bank:

```prompt
My card 4149 6090 1234 5678 was charged €35 twice for the gym. My banking app password is Daniel1987!, can you log in and check? My phone number is +353 87 123 4567.
```

Claude will help with the complaint. But there are three things in this message that shouldn't have gone into the chat. Which ones?
:::

::: step explain
A chat message is more like a letter than a conversation out loud. It's saved in your history, anyone who opens your account can see it, and with the setting on (lesson b6.1) it may be used for training.

So the rule is simple: don't send anything to a chat that you wouldn't write on a postcard.
:::

::: step explain
Don't send to a chat:

- *secrets*: passwords, PINs, SMS codes, access keys for services;
- full card numbers, account numbers, passport or national ID numbers;
- *other people's personal data*: names, phone numbers, addresses, diagnoses of colleagues, clients, family — especially children.

Your own data, too: only as much as the task needs.
:::

options:
  - text: "An SMS code your bank sent you"
    correct: true
    why: "An SMS code is a key to your account. You don't give it to anyone: not people, not programs. And Claude doesn't need it to help you."
  - text: "Your full card number, so Claude can “check the payment”"
    correct: true
    why: "Claude can't see your bank and won't check a payment by card number. But the number will stay in your chat history. For a complaint, the last 4 digits or even a placeholder are enough."
  - text: "A class list with pupils' dates of birth and parents' phone numbers"
    correct: true
    why: "That's other people's personal data, and children's at that. They didn't consent. If you need a table, replace names with “Pupil 1, Pupil 2” and remove the phone numbers."
  - text: "The question “How do I write a complaint to my bank about a double charge?”"
    correct: false
    why: "There's no personal data here, just the task itself. That's exactly right: describe the situation without giving away anything extra."
  - text: "Your holiday: “I'm going to Barcelona for a week in July, help me plan a route”"
    correct: false
    why: "The city and month are needed for the task and don't identify anyone. That's normal context. Your passport and booking number aren't needed for a route."
hint: "Ask yourself about each item: if a stranger read this, would anyone be harmed? And does Claude need it to answer?"
:::

::: step explain
The good news: Claude almost never needs real data. It needs the situation.

To *anonymise* a text means to replace personal details with *placeholders*: “[NAME]”, “[PHONE]”, “card ending 5678”. Like in a law textbook: “Party A signed a contract with Party B.” The meaning is clear, but no one can be identified.
:::

    why: "To explain test results, Claude needs the numbers and the age. The full name and record number add nothing to the answer."
  - left: "A bank statement with your account number"
    right: "A table of “date, category, amount” with no numbers or names"
    why: "For a look at your spending, amounts and categories matter. The account number and payees' names can be removed or replaced."
  - left: "A work email from a client with their signature and phone number"
    right: "“[CLIENT] writes: …”, signature and phone number removed"
    why: "The reply depends on what the email says, not on the client's contact details. Their data isn't yours to share."
  - left: "A nursery letter with your child's name"
    right: "“My son, 5” instead of his first and last name"
    why: "The age helps Claude give suitable advice. The child's first and last name aren't needed for that."
hint: "For each task, keep only what the answer can't do without."
:::

::: step explain
Anonymising is easy in three steps:

1. Find names, phone numbers, addresses, document, card and account numbers.
2. Replace them with placeholders: “[NAME]”, “[PHONE]”, “card ending 5678”.
3. Reread it: can Claude help with this text? Usually, yes.

You'll add the real details to the finished answer yourself, on your own device.
:::

::: exercise single_choice
id: "b6.2.e4"
type: "single_choice"
question: "Daniel anonymised his prompt. Which version is both safe and useful?"
options:
  - text: "“Help me write a complaint to my bank: on 3 October my card (ending 5678) was charged €35 twice for the gym. I want one of the charges refunded. Signature: [NAME], [PHONE].”"
    correct: true
    why: "It has everything a complaint needs: what happened, when, how much and what he wants. The last 4 digits are enough for the bank to find the card. Daniel will add his name and phone number himself."
  - text: "“Help me with my bank”"
    correct: false
    why: "Safe, but useless: Claude doesn't know what happened. Anonymising means removing personal details, not the situation itself."
  - text: "“Card 4149 6090 1234 5678, password Daniel1987! — check what's going on with the charge”"
    correct: false
    why: "A password and a full card number are the most dangerous things you could send. Claude can't log in to the bank anyway. If a password has been exposed anywhere, change it."
  - text: "“Help me write a complaint. My number is +353 87 123 4567, call me if anything comes up”"
    correct: false
    why: "Claude doesn't make calls, and the number will stay in your history. And the key part is missing: what the charge was and what Daniel wants."
hint: "Look for the version that keeps the situation (what, when, how much) and replaces the personal details."
:::

::: step summary
**Send the situation to the chat, not your personal data.**

Never send passwords, codes or full document and card numbers. Replace other people's names and contact details with placeholders, and add the real details to the finished text yourself.

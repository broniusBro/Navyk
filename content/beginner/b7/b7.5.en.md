::: step hook
Lucy from Brighton is writing wedding toasts, and by lunchtime she sees her limit is almost used up.

She asked 15 short questions in a row, explained who the bride and groom were every single time, and did it all in the chat where she'd been choosing a dress the week before.

What could she have done differently? There are 5 simple techniques.
:::

::: step explain
A quick reminder: your *limit* is how much you can use Claude in a given period, like the data allowance on your phone.

How fast you use it depends on the length of your messages and files, the length of the chat, tools like web search, the model and the “effort” level. You can see how much is left in {{data.ui_paths.settings_usage}}.
:::

::: step explain
**5 techniques from Anthropic's advice:**

1. **Combine** related questions into one message.
2. **Give context up front**, so there's no need for follow-ups.
3. **Review your message** before sending.
4. **Put files you use again and again in a project**: there they use less of your limit.
5. **New task, new chat**: a long chat costs more.
:::

::: step summary
**Your limit lasts longer when one good prompt replaces five follow-ups.**

5 techniques: combine questions, give context up front, review before sending, keep files you reuse in a project, and start a new task in a new chat.

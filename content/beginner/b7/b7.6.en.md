::: step hook
You've made it to the final of the Beginner level!

You've covered chats, prompts, files, projects, memory, privacy and checking answers. Today we'll put it all together on two real tasks:

- going through your spending for the month;
- making a plan to learn a new skill.

First a quick warm-up, then two practice tasks with feedback from Claude.
:::

::: step explain
Any task can be done in four moves:

1. **Choose the tool:** a regular chat, a chat with a file, or a project.
2. **Prepare the data:** remove personal details Claude doesn't need.
3. **Write the prompt:** goal, context, format and, if needed, steps, a role and an example.
4. **Check the answer:** figures, sources, “I don't know”.
:::

::: exercise match_pairs
id: "b7.6.e1"
type: "match_pairs"
question: "Move 1. Match each task with the right tool."
pairs:
  - left: "Write a one-off message congratulating a colleague"
    right: "A regular chat"
    why: "A one-off task with no documents. A good prompt in a new chat, and you're done."
  - left: "Understand what your broadband contract that arrived today actually says"
    right: "A chat with the file attached"
    why: "The answer has to be based on a specific document. Attach it and ask Claude to back up its conclusions with quotes."
  - left: "Prepare for an English exam over six months using the same materials"
    right: "A project with files and instructions"
    why: "You'll come back to the topic many times. A project keeps the materials and instructions for every new chat, so you don't have to explain everything again."
hint: "Ask yourself: is the task one-off or recurring? Does the answer need a specific document?"
:::

::: exercise single_choice
id: "b7.6.e2"
type: "single_choice"
question: "Jonas from Utrecht wants to go through his September card statement and see where his money goes. What's the best way to start?"
options:
  - text: "Download the statement, remove the card and account numbers, open a new chat and attach the file"
    correct: true
    why: "A one-off review of one document: a new chat with the file is enough. Claude doesn't need the card or account number for the analysis, so they're removed beforehand. Remember the module on privacy?"
  - text: "Carry on in the chat where he was choosing a present for his wife yesterday and paste the statement there"
    correct: false
    why: "New task, new chat. The conversation about the present will get in the way and eat into his limit."
  - text: "Ask Claude without the statement: “Where does people's money usually go?”"
    correct: false
    why: "Without data, Claude will answer about “the average person”, not about Jonas. And it would have to make up exact figures, which is a direct route to a hallucination."
  - text: "Send the statement along with his banking login and password so Claude can look at everything itself"
    correct: false
    why: "Never send passwords in a chat. For this review, the statement itself without card numbers is all that's needed."
hint: "Think about three things: which tool, which data isn't needed, and whether the chat should be new or old."
:::

::: exercise find_error
id: "b7.6.e3"
type: "find_error"
question: "Move 2. Jonas has written a prompt for his statement. Tap the fragments that shouldn't be sent or that will make the answer worse."
prompt: "Here's the statement for my card 4405 1234 5678 9012. Sort my September spending into categories. Answer confidently, even if something's unclear."
segments:
  - text: "Here's the statement for my card "
    weak: false
    why: "A fine start: it's clear what the document is."
  - text: "4405 1234 5678 9012. "
    weak: true
    why: "Never send a full card number in a chat. It isn't needed to review spending. Just write “for my card” or keep only the last 4 digits."
  - text: "Sort my September spending into categories. "
    weak: false
    why: "The goal is clear. You could strengthen it with a format (a table) and context (why), but this fragment is good on its own."
  - text: "Answer confidently, even if something's unclear."
    weak: true
    why: "This bans saying “I don't know”. If a transaction on the statement is unclear, Claude will guess the category, and the totals will end up wrong. Better: “list any unclear transactions separately”."
hint: "Look for two problems: privacy and a ban on honesty."
:::

::: exercise build_prompt
id: "b7.6.e4"
type: "build_prompt"
question: "Move 3. Build a strong prompt for Jonas's statement from the blocks. Leave out the blocks you don't need."
blocks:
  - id: "b1"
    text: "You're a careful personal budgeting assistant."
    role: "role"
    why: "A one-sentence role sets the tone: careful, to the point, no judging the spending."
  - id: "b2"
    text: "Go through the attached September statement: sort the spending into categories and total each one."
    role: "goal"
    why: "The main action and result: categories and totals. Without this block it's unclear what to do with the file."
  - id: "b3"
    text: "I want to see where I could save €100 a month so I can put money aside for a summer holiday."
    role: "context"
    why: "This is the “why”. Knowing the goal, Claude will pay attention to spending that can be cut, not just add things up."
  - id: "b4"
    text: "Answer: a table “category — amount — % of spending”, with 3 specific ideas for saving underneath. List any transactions you can't assign to a category separately; don't guess."
    role: "format"
    why: "The format is described as “what to do”, and unclear transactions aren't guessed. That's the permission to say “I don't know”."
  - id: "b5"
    text: "My banking app login: jonas_88."
    role: "noise"
    why: "A bank login isn't needed for the review and shouldn't end up in a chat. Unnecessary personal data is always unnecessary."
  - id: "b6"
    text: "You know what I mean, make it look nice."
    role: "noise"
    why: "Fails the colleague test: “nice” says nothing about the format. The block with the table already describes what the answer should look like."
target_order: ["b1", "b2", "b3", "b4"]
hint: "The order: who Claude is, what to do, why, what the answer should look like. Logins and empty phrases aren't needed."
:::

::: exercise multi_choice
id: "b7.6.e5"
type: "multi_choice"
question: "Move 4. Claude has sent back a spending table. What's sensible to do next? Select all."
options:
  - text: "Check a couple of the biggest amounts against the statement"
    correct: true
    why: "Figures are where mistakes are most likely. A quick spot check shows whether you can trust the table."
  - text: "Ask Claude to show which transactions it put under “Eating out”"
    correct: true
    why: "This asks for support: you can see what the conclusion is based on and spot a wrong category straight away."
  - text: "Look at the separate list of unclear transactions and sort them yourself"
    correct: true
    why: "You asked Claude not to guess, so now it's your turn. You know exactly what that transfer on 12 September was."
  - text: "Send the table straight to his wife as an accurate report, without looking"
    correct: false
    why: "Check first, then send. If there's a mistake in the table, an awkward conversation is guaranteed."
  - text: "Ask a new question about a lasagne recipe in the same chat"
    correct: false
    why: "New task, new chat. The recipe will get in the way here, and the spending table will get in the way of the recipe."
hint: "Check the figures, ask for support and don't mix tasks."
:::

::: step explain
Now for two real practice tasks. Navyk will send each of your prompts to Claude for feedback.

Don't attach a statement or include real figures if you'd rather not: you can make them up. What matters is that all four moves show in your prompt: goal, context, format and checking.
:::

::: exercise write_prompt
id: "b7.6.e6"
type: "write_prompt"
question: "Task 1. Write a prompt asking Claude to go through your spending statement for the month (imagine the file is attached). Include a goal, context and format, and ask Claude not to guess anything unclear. Don't include card or account numbers."
placeholder: "Go through the attached statement for…"
ideas:
  - "Why do you want the review: saving up, cutting back, just curious?"
  - "How much you want to save and by when"
  - "What the answer should look like: table, list, ideas"
  - "What to do with unclear transactions"
  - "Role: personal budgeting assistant"
min_chars: 80
hint: "Go through the moves: what to do with the statement, why you need it, what the answer should look like and what to do with transactions Claude can't make sense of. No card or account numbers."
review:
  task: "The final practice of the Beginner level. The learner writes a prompt for an attached monthly spending statement (the file is implied). A strong prompt: makes clear what to do with the statement (R1); explains why: a savings goal, an amount, a deadline or another reason (R2); sets the format: a table, categories, totals, ideas (R3); has a criterion for a good and honest answer: unclear transactions are listed separately rather than guessed, or “if you're not sure, say so” (R4). A role and steps are a bonus. If the learner included a card number, account number, password or login, R10 applies: first gently warn them and don't repeat that data. This is the celebratory level final: start with what's already good."
  criteria: ["R1", "R2", "R3", "R4"]
  reference_prompt: |
    You're a careful personal budgeting assistant. Go through the attached statement for my card for September: sort the spending into categories (groceries, eating out, transport, subscriptions, clothes, other) and total each one. I want to see where I could save €100 a month so I can put money aside for a summer holiday. Answer: a table “category — amount — % of total spending”, with 3 specific ideas for saving underneath, each with a rough amount. List any transactions you can't assign to a category separately, with the date and amount; don't guess. If the statement is missing something you'd need to draw a conclusion, say so directly.
  show_reference: true
  fallback_feedback:
    - "Goal: is it clear what to do with the statement?"
    - "Context: does it explain why you want the review: an amount, a deadline, a reason?"
    - "Format: does it describe what the answer should look like: a table, categories, ideas?"
    - "Honesty: does it say what to do with unclear transactions instead of guessing?"
    - "Privacy: is the prompt free of card and account numbers, logins and passwords?"
:::

::: exercise write_prompt
id: "b7.6.e7"
type: "write_prompt"
question: "Task 2. You want to learn a new skill in 2 months: for example, Excel for work, Polish for a move abroad or phone photography. Write a prompt for a learning plan. Think about where it's more convenient to keep this task: in a regular chat or in a project."
placeholder: "In 2 months I want to learn…"
ideas:
  - "Which skill, and why you want it"
  - "Your current level"
  - "How much time you have each week"
  - "A week-by-week plan, steps"
  - "An example of an exercise you like"
  - "If you're not sure about a resource, say so"
min_chars: 80
hint: "Describe the skill and why you need it, your current level and how much time you have. Ask for a week-by-week plan in a clear format. If you ask for books or courses, allow Claude to say honestly if it isn't sure they exist."
review:
  task: "The final practice of the Beginner level. The learner writes a prompt for a plan to learn a new skill in 2 months. A strong prompt: makes clear which skill and what result is needed (R1); has context: why the skill matters, the current level, how much time per week (R2); sets the format: a week-by-week plan, length, layout (R3); makes clear what counts as a good plan, e.g. “realistic for 3 hours a week”, and guards against invented resources: “if you're not sure a course or book exists, say so” (R4). Bonuses: a role, steps, an example of the kind of exercise wanted, a mention that the plan will live in a project. This is the celebratory level final: start with what's already good and acknowledge their progress."
  criteria: ["R1", "R2", "R3", "R4"]
  reference_prompt: |
    You're an experienced teacher of Polish for adults. I'm moving to Wrocław in 2 months and by then I want to be able to get by in shops, at the doctor's and at the bank. Right now I only know the alphabet and a few phrases. I can study for 30 minutes on weekdays and an hour at weekends. Make a week-by-week plan: for each week, a topic, 3–4 specific exercises and one real-life situation to practise, such as “asking for directions”. If you recommend apps, books or channels, only name ones you're sure exist; otherwise, say so. Answer as a table by week, with no unnecessary theory. I'll save this plan in a “Polish” project and come back to it every week.
  show_reference: true
  fallback_feedback:
    - "Goal: is it clear which skill and what result you want in 2 months?"
    - "Context: does it say why, your current level and how much time you have each week?"
    - "Format: are you asking for a week-by-week plan in a clear format?"
    - "Honesty: have you allowed Claude to say if it isn't sure a book or course exists?"
    - "Tool: have you considered that a project works well for a 2-month task?"
:::

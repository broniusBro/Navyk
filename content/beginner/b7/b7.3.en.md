::: step hook
You already know how to write a strong prompt. But in practice, most weak prompts “break” in the same three places.

```prompt
Write a summary
```

Claude won't know: a summary of what? An article? Or a personal summary for your CV? For which job? How long? Let's learn to spot gaps like these in a couple of seconds.
:::

::: step explain
Check your prompt with three questions:

1. **Goal.** What exactly should be done, and why?
2. **Context.** What facts do only you know: who it's for, what the situation is, what data there is?
3. **Format.** What should the answer look like: length, layout, tone?

It's like ordering a cake: “a cake” (goal), “for my daughter's 7th birthday, she loves strawberries” (context), “for 10 people, with writing on top” (format).
:::

::: exercise match_pairs
id: "b7.3.e1"
type: "match_pairs"
question: "Match each weak prompt with the main thing it's missing."
pairs:
  - left: "“I need something about a holiday in Georgia”"
    right: "Goal: it's unclear what to do"
    why: "A route plan? A packing list? A budget? A comparison of cities? Claude doesn't know what result you need, so it guesses."
  - left: "“Write an email asking for a discount, 5 sentences, polite”"
    right: "Context: no facts about the situation"
    why: "What to do and in what form is clear. But who's the email to, a discount on what, and why do you deserve it? Without facts you'll get a template."
  - left: "“I teach Year 6, tomorrow's lesson is on volcanoes, and the kids are tired by Friday. Help me with the start of the lesson”"
    right: "Format: it's unclear what the answer should look like"
    why: "The situation and goal are clear. But what's needed: a riddle, an experiment, a video, a 5-minute plan? Say what result you expect and how much of it."
hint: "Each prompt has two of the three parts. Find the one that's missing."
:::

::: exercise find_error
id: "b7.3.e2"
type: "find_error"
question: "Lukas from Antwerp writes to Claude. Tap the fragments where the goal, context or format “break”."
prompt: "Help with some text for the website about our services, you know what I mean, do it like usual but better."
segments:
  - text: "Help with some text "
    weak: true
    why: "The goal is vague: write from scratch, edit, shorten? “Help with some text” could mean anything. Better to use an action verb: “write”, “shorten”, “rewrite”."
  - text: "for the website "
    weak: false
    why: "A good detail: it's clear where the text will go. You could name the page (home, “About us”), but the direction is set."
  - text: "about our services, "
    weak: true
    why: "Which services? Who are the customers? How are you different from competitors? That's context only Lukas knows."
  - text: "you know what I mean, "
    weak: true
    why: "Claude doesn't know: it has never seen the website or the company. This phrase fails the colleague test."
  - text: "do it like usual but better."
    weak: true
    why: "There's no format: what length, what tone, which sections? Claude doesn't know what “usual” is, and “better” with no criteria says nothing."
hint: "Check each fragment with three questions: what to do, what facts, what the answer should look like."
:::

::: exercise single_choice
id: "b7.3.e3"
type: "single_choice"
question: "Sofia writes: “Make me a 4-week workout plan as a table: day, exercise, time.” Which mistake isn't there, and which is the main one?"
options:
  - text: "The goal and format are there, but context is missing: fitness level, what she's training for, where she works out"
    correct: true
    why: "Right. A plan for a beginner at home and one for someone training for a half marathon are completely different. Add a couple of facts: “I run once a week, I want to run 10 km by spring, I train in the park”."
  - text: "The goal is missing: it's unclear what to do"
    correct: false
    why: "The goal is actually clear: make a 4-week workout plan. What's missing is facts about Sofia herself."
  - text: "The format is missing: it's unclear what the answer will look like"
    correct: false
    why: "The format is clearly set: a table with day, exercise and time. That's the prompt's strong point."
  - text: "The prompt is perfect, there's nothing to add"
    correct: false
    why: "Claude doesn't know Sofia's level or her goal. It'll write a plan for “the average person”, which could turn out too easy or dangerously hard."
hint: "Find “what to do” and “in what form” in the prompt. Which of the three is missing?"
:::

::: exercise single_choice
id: "b7.3.e4"
type: "single_choice"
question: "Which format instruction will best tell Claude what the answer should look like?"
options:
  - text: "“Answer: 5 bullet points, one line each, in plain words, casual tone”"
    correct: true
    why: "It describes the result you want: how many, how long and in what tone. Claude doesn't have to guess. You'll remember this from the lesson on format: say what to do."
  - text: "“Just don't make it long, and none of those lists of yours”"
    correct: false
    why: "These are bans. Claude knows what you don't want, but not what you do want: one paragraph, two, a table? Better to describe what you want directly."
  - text: "“Do it properly”"
    correct: false
    why: "Everyone's idea of “properly” is different. No format is set here at all."
hint: "Look for the option that describes the answer you want rather than banning the one you don't."
:::

::: exercise improve_prompt
id: "b7.3.e5"
type: "improve_prompt"
question: "Fix the prompt so it has a goal, context and format. Make up the details yourself."
weak_prompt: "Write something about the meeting"
placeholder: "Write an announcement for… about the meeting…"
ideas:
  - "What kind of meeting: parents', residents', work?"
  - "Who will read it?"
  - "What do you need from the readers?"
  - "Where will it be posted: group chat, noticeboard, email?"
  - "What length and tone?"
min_chars: 50
hint: "Answer three questions. Goal: what text you need (an announcement, a reminder, an agenda). Context: what meeting, when, what about, who the readers are. Format: where it'll be posted, what length and tone."
review:
  task: "The learner is fixing the prompt “Write something about the meeting”, which has no goal, context or format. The lesson teaches spotting and fixing exactly these three mistakes (P1–P3). The learner may make up the details. A strong prompt: makes clear what text is needed and why (R1); has facts about the meeting and the readers (R2); sets where it'll be posted, the length and tone (R3); makes clear what counts as a good result, e.g. what the reader should do (R4). In your feedback, name directly which of the three parts are already there and which is missing."
  criteria: ["R1", "R2", "R3", "R4"]
  reference_prompt: |
    Write an announcement for our building's residents' WhatsApp group. The meeting is on Thursday at 7 pm in the courtyard, or in the entrance hall of block A if it rains. Topic: choosing a contractor to repair the roof, and there'll be 3 quotes to compare. We need at least 30 people to turn up, otherwise no decision can be made. Friendly tone, no officialese. 4–5 sentences, and at the end ask people to reply “+” if they're coming.
  show_reference: true
  fallback_feedback:
    - "Goal: is it clear what text you need (an announcement, a reminder, an agenda) and why?"
    - "Context: are there facts about the meeting: what, when, where, what about?"
    - "Context: does it say who will read it?"
    - "Format: does it say where it'll be posted, the length and the tone?"
    - "Is it clear what the reader should do after reading?"
:::

::: step summary
**Before sending, ask your prompt three questions: what's the goal, what's the context, what's the format.**

Add whatever's missing. That way you'll fix most weak prompts in half a minute.

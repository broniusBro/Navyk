::: step hook
You don't hire a lorry to pop to the corner shop. And you don't move a sofa on a scooter.

Claude works the same way: there isn't just one “brain” inside, there are several. Some are quick and light, others slow and deep. In 5 minutes you'll learn to pick the right one for the job.
:::

::: step explain
These “brains” are called *models*. There are three main families:

- **Haiku**: the fastest and lightest, for short answers and simple summaries;
- **Sonnet**: the all-rounder, good for most things;
- **Opus**: for complex tasks that need a lot of thinking.

The “heavier” the model, the more of your limit it uses. Not sure what to choose? Start with Sonnet.
:::

::: step explain
There's a second dial: *Effort*. It's how much Claude thinks before answering. Like an exam: you answer an easy question straight away but spend longer on a hard problem.

Click the model name next to the send button and choose **Effort**. Levels go from **Low** (quick) to **Max** (deepest), and the options depend on the model. The recommended one is marked **Default**.
:::

::: step summary
**The model is Claude's “brain”; Effort is how much it thinks. Simple task: lighter and faster. Hard task: heavier and deeper.**

Start with Sonnet and the default Effort. Only step up when a task is genuinely hard: that way your limit lasts longer.

Next up: plans and limits.
:::

::: step hook
Tom is getting ready for a trip to New York and asks Claude:

```prompt
What's the euro to US dollar exchange rate today?
```

```claude
I don't have today's rate: my knowledge only goes up to the date my training ended. I can search the web for it. You'll also find the current rate on the European Central Bank's website and in your banking app.
```

Claude knows a lot, but not everything. In 4 minutes we'll look at why, and what to do about it.
:::

::: step explain
Claude learned from a huge amount of text, but only up to a certain day. That day is called its *knowledge cutoff*.

Picture someone who spent six months at a polar research station with no internet. They know everything that happened before they left. But they've missed the latest news, prices and exchange rates.
:::

options:
  - text: "The new model came out after Claude's knowledge cutoff, so it doesn't know about it"
    correct: true
    why: "Yes. Claude's training doesn't include anything that happened after that date. Like the polar researcher who's just got back and hasn't caught up on the news yet."
  - text: "Claude hides new models on purpose to avoid advertising"
    correct: false
    why: "There's no conspiracy. Claude simply hasn't seen any texts about events after its cutoff."
  - text: "Claude is broken and you should contact support"
    correct: false
    why: "Everything is working as intended. Knowledge from training always lags a little behind real life; that's normal for any AI model."
  - text: "You worded the question wrong"
    correct: false
    why: "Wording has nothing to do with it. Even a perfect question can't help Claude know something that wasn't in its training. Web search can, and that's next."
hint: "Think of the polar researcher. What do they know about things that happened while they were at the station?"
:::

::: step explain
The good news: for most tasks the date doesn't matter. Emails, translations, explanations and ideas don't depend on it.

For anything recent you need *web search*: Claude searches the internet itself and shows links to its sources. It's like asking a friend to “just google it”. You can say so directly: “Search the web for…”

Recent means exchange rates, prices, timetables, news, match results.
:::

::: exercise match_pairs
id: "b1.2.e2"
type: "match_pairs"
question: "Match each question with what it needs."
pairs:
  - left: "“Explain in simple terms what compound interest is”"
    right: "Claude's own knowledge is enough: the topic hasn't changed in years"
    why: "How compound interest works has been known for a long time. What matters here is explaining it well, and Claude can do that without the internet."
  - left: "“What's the zloty to euro rate today?”"
    right: "Needs web search: the number changes every day"
    why: "Exchange rates change daily. Without a search Claude doesn't know them, and guessing numbers is a bad idea when money is involved."
  - left: "“What films are on at the cinema this weekend?”"
    right: "Needs web search: listings change every week"
    why: "This weekend's listings appeared recently, so they're definitely not in Claude's knowledge. Let it search the web and give you a link to the cinema's website."
  - left: "“Write a housewarming message for a colleague”"
    right: "Hardly needs knowledge of the world; your details matter"
    why: "You don't need the latest news to write a warm message. You need details from you: your colleague's name and how well you know each other."
hint: "Ask yourself about each question: will the answer be different in a week?"
:::

::: exercise compare_answers
id: "b1.2.e3"
type: "compare_answers"
variant: "why_better"
question: "Nina asked the same thing twice: without web search and with it. What makes the second answer useful?"
cards:
  - id: "A"
    label: "Without web search"
    prompt: "What films are showing in Dublin cinemas tonight?"
    answer: |
      I don't have access to current listings: my knowledge only goes up to the date my training ended. It's best to check showtimes on the cinemas' websites or in a ticketing app.
  - id: "B"
    label: "With web search"
    prompt: "Search the web for what films are showing in Dublin cinemas tonight. Give me links."
    answer: |
      I searched and found listings on three cinema websites. Seven films are showing after 7 pm tonight; you'll find the full list with showtimes and prices at the links below.
      [1] City-centre cinema website
      [2] Shopping-centre cinema website
      [3] Northside cinema website
      Showtimes sometimes change on the day, so check the link before you head out.
options:
  - text: "Claude found up-to-date information and gave links so you can check it"
    correct: true
    why: "Exactly. The search brought in fresh data, and the links let you check it against the original source: the cinema's own website."
  - text: "Claude got smarter in the second answer"
    correct: false
    why: "It's the same Claude. The difference is that the second time it had fresh information from the internet."
  - text: "The first answer is bad because Claude lied"
    correct: false
    why: "Quite the opposite: the first answer is honest. Claude said plainly that it didn't know the listings and suggested where to look. That's better than making up showtimes."
  - text: "You can trust an answer with search results without checking"
    correct: false
    why: "A search can also turn up an outdated or inaccurate page. That's what the links are for: open the source if something depends on the answer."
hint: "Compare where Claude got its information in each answer and what you can do with the links."
:::

id: "b1.2.e4"

options:
  - text: "“Search the web for this year's application deadline for the summer Polish course in Kraków. Give me a link to the official website.”"
    correct: true
    why: "It asks for a web search, names the specific course and year, and asks for a link to the original source. You can confirm the date on the official website, where it should be correct."
  - text: "“When's the deadline for courses in Kraków?”"
    correct: false
    why: "This year's deadline was published recently, so without a search Claude may not know it or may give you last year's. And it's unclear which courses you mean."
  - text: "“Are you sure about the dates? Tell me only the truth!”"
    correct: false
    why: "Demanding the truth doesn't help: if Claude doesn't have recent data, no amount of pressure will create it. You need a search and a link to the source."
hint: "For a recent fact you need two things: a web search and a source you can open yourself."
:::

::: step summary
**Claude's knowledge stops at its cutoff date. For anything recent, use web search with links.**

Ask yourself: will the answer be different in a week? If so, ask Claude to “Search the web for…” and open the sources.

::: step hook
Sam has added his price list and delivery policy to his project, The Tea Shelf. A customer asks about a discount:

```claude
Dear Valued Customer, thank you for contacting our company. Unfortunately, we are unable to offer a discount at the present time; however, we greatly value your interest in our products and look forward to a long and mutually beneficial relationship…
```

The prices are right, but it sounds like a bank's automated letter. What's missing?
:::

::: step explain
What's missing is *project instructions*: a standing briefing on how Claude should work in this project. Like a briefing sheet for a new shop assistant: “greet people by name, keep replies short, if someone complains, apologise first”.

Project knowledge answers “what to know”, while instructions answer “how to work”. They apply in every chat in the project, and only there.
:::

::: exercise single_choice
id: "b4.3.e1"
type: "single_choice"
question: "Which of these is best written into The Tea Shelf's project instructions rather than its project knowledge?"
options:
  - text: "“Reply warmly, politely and briefly, 2–4 sentences, and sign off as ‘Sam’”"
    correct: true
    why: "This is a working rule: tone, length, sign-off. It belongs in the instructions so that Claude follows it in every chat."
  - text: "A price table for 40 kinds of tea"
    correct: false
    why: "This is reference material, in other words “what to know”. The price list goes into the project knowledge as a file."
  - text: "Today's question from a customer, Emma, about her order"
    correct: false
    why: "This is a one-off task for a specific chat. You write it as a message, not into the standing instructions."
hint: "Instructions answer “how to work”, knowledge answers “what to know”, and a chat message answers “what to do right now”."
:::

::: step explain
Where to set them: on the project page, click {{data.ui_paths.project_instructions_set}}, write your text and click {{data.ui_paths.project_instructions_save}}.

Good instructions have four parts:

- *role*: who to be (“you're a shop assistant's helper”);
- *context and why*: what the business is and why things are done this way;
- *tone*;
- *format*: the length and shape of the answer.

Can't see the button? Feature access depends on your plan: check yours in Claude's help centre.
:::

::: exercise match_pairs
id: "b4.3.e2"
type: "match_pairs"
question: "Match each part of the instructions with an example from The Tea Shelf project."
pairs:
  - left: "Role"
    right: "“You help Sam answer customers of his tea shop”"
    why: "Even one sentence about the role sets the focus: Claude answers as a shop assistant's helper, not as an encyclopaedia of tea."
  - left: "Context and why"
    right: "“Customers mostly message us on Instagram from their phones, so replies should take 10 seconds to read”"
    why: "The reason helps Claude understand the point of the rule. Knowing people read on their phones, it'll avoid long paragraphs even in situations you didn't describe."
  - left: "Tone"
    right: "“Polite, warm and simple, like a small family-run shop”"
    why: "The tone is described with a clear image. That cures the “bank's automated letter” from the start of the lesson."
  - left: "Format"
    right: "“2–4 sentences, signed ‘Sam, The Tea Shelf’ at the end”"
    why: "The length and shape of the answer are specific. Claude won't have to guess what “short” means."
hint: "Role is “who you are”, context is “what the business is and why”, tone is “how to sound”, format is “how long and what shape”."
:::

::: step explain
Two techniques make instructions stronger.

**Explain why.** Not just “no long replies”, but “customers read on their phones”. Claude follows a rule better when it understands the point of it.

**Say what to do, not what not to do.** Instead of “don't use corporate jargon”, write “use plain words, as if you're chatting”.
:::

::: exercise find_error
id: "b4.3.e3"
type: "find_error"
question: "Anna in Utrecht has written instructions for her “Family budget” project. Tap every weak spot."
prompt: "You're our family's financial assistant. Don't write too much. Don't use complicated words. Help us cut costs, because we're saving up to renovate our home by summer."
segments:
  - text: "You're our family's financial assistant. "
    weak: false
    why: "A good role: Claude understands it's working for a specific family, not giving a lecture on economics."
  - text: "Don't write too much. "
    weak: true
    why: "A ban with no measure: how much is “too much”? Better to say what to do: “answer in 3–5 bullet points”."
  - text: "Don't use complicated words. "
    weak: true
    why: "Another ban. Better to describe what you want: “explain in plain words, as you would to a friend with no finance background”."
  - text: "Help us cut costs, because we're saving up to renovate our home by summer."
    weak: false
    why: "A great part: there's a goal and a reason. Knowing about the renovation by summer, Claude can suggest how much to put aside each month on its own."
hint: "Look for sentences that start with “Don't”. What exactly does Anna want instead?"
:::

::: exercise build_prompt
id: "b4.3.e4"
type: "build_prompt"
question: "Put together the instructions for a project called “Leaving Cert History revision”. Leave out any blocks you don't need."
blocks:
  - id: "b1"
    text: "You're a history tutor for a student in her final year of secondary school."
    role: "role"
    why: "The role sets the level and style: Claude explains like a tutor rather than writing an academic paper."
  - id: "b2"
    text: "She's sitting her Leaving Cert exam in June and remembers things best through causes and effects, so link dates to the events that led to them."
    role: "context"
    why: "Context with a reason: the deadline, the goal and how the student learns best. Claude can then adapt its explanations by itself."
  - id: "b3"
    text: "Explain things in plain words and end every answer with one question to test her."
    role: "goal"
    why: "This is the main working rule: how to explain and what to do at the end. The question at the end turns the chat into practice."
  - id: "b4"
    text: "Keep answers under 150 words and put key dates in bold."
    role: "format"
    why: "The format is specific: length and how to highlight the key points. Handy for revising before the exam."
  - id: "b5"
    text: "Be the best tutor in the world!!!"
    role: "noise"
    why: "It sounds upbeat but says nothing: Claude won't learn what exactly to do better. Specific rules work; exclamation marks don't."
  - id: "b6"
    text: "Project name: “Leaving Cert History”."
    role: "noise"
    why: "Claude can't see the project name, and in the instructions it adds nothing. It's a label for you."
target_order: ["b1", "b2", "b3", "b4"]
hint: "Start with who Claude should be, then explain the situation and why, then the working rules, and finish with the format. Set aside blocks with nothing specific in them."
:::

::: step explain
Now it's your turn. A real-world task: instructions for a project called “Replying to online shop customers”. Make up a shop or use one you know: clothes, cosmetics, pet supplies.

Write for any customer question, not just one. Navyk will send your instructions to Claude for feedback.
:::

::: exercise write_prompt
id: "b4.3.e5"
type: "write_prompt"
question: "Write instructions for a project called “Replying to online shop customers”: role, context with a reason, tone and answer format. Don't include real phone numbers, addresses or customer names."
placeholder: "You help me reply to customers of my shop…"
ideas:
  - "What kind of shop is it, and where does it operate?"
  - "Where do customers message you, and why does that matter?"
  - "What tone should replies have?"
  - "How long is a reply, and how does it end?"
  - "What if the answer isn't in the project files?"
min_chars: 80
hint: "Go through the four parts: who Claude should be, what the shop is and why your rules matter, how to sound, and how long the reply is and what shape. Say what to do, not what not to do."
review:
  task: "The learner is writing project instructions (standing rules for every chat in the project), not a one-off prompt. Project: “Replying to online shop customers”; the learner may invent the shop. The lesson teaches four parts: role, context with a reason (“why”), tone, answer format; rules are phrased as “what to do”. Don't require a specific customer question: the instructions should work for any question. Bonus (don't mark down if missing): a rule for when the answer isn't in the project files."
  criteria: ["R1", "R2", "R3", "R4"]
  reference_prompt: |
    You help me, the owner of Lavender, an online natural cosmetics shop (based in Dublin, shipping across Ireland and the EU), reply to customers.

    Customers message us on Instagram and WhatsApp, mostly from their phones, so a reply should take a few seconds to read. Many are buying from us for the first time, and the first reply decides whether they place an order.

    Reply politely, warmly and simply, like an assistant in a small boutique. If a customer is unhappy, first acknowledge the problem and offer a concrete next step: a replacement, a refund or a discount on their next order.

    Take prices, delivery times and the returns policy only from the project files. If the information isn't there, say so and offer to check with me.

    Format: 2–4 sentences, no bullet points, signed “Marina, Lavender” at the end.
  show_reference: true
  fallback_feedback:
    - "Is there a role: what Claude's job is in this project?"
    - "Is it clear what the shop is, and does it explain why your rules matter (e.g. “people read on their phones”)?"
    - "Is the tone described with a clear image, e.g. “like an assistant in a small boutique”?"
    - "Is the format set: reply length, sign-off, bullet points or continuous text?"
    - "Are the rules phrased as “what to do”, not just “don't”?"
    - "Would the instructions work for any customer question, not just one?"
    - "Is the text free of real phone numbers, addresses and customer names?"
:::

::: step summary
**Project knowledge is what to know. Project instructions are how to work.**

Good instructions: role, context with a reason, tone and format. Say what to do, and explain why.

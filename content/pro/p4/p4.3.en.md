::: step hook
Tom from Dublin launched a support bot for a broadband provider. A customer writes: “The internet's down AGAIN!!!” The bot replies:

```claude
The internet is a global system of interconnected computer networks. There are many possible reasons for a lost connection, ranging from hardware faults to…
```

Technically correct, practically useless. The bot doesn't know who it is or why it's there.
:::

::: step explain
In the API, a request has a separate `system` field: the *system prompt*. It's a standing briefing for Claude for the whole conversation: who to be and how to work.

`messages` holds the conversation itself: what the user writes. It's like project instructions in the chat, except your code sets them.
:::

::: step explain
```python
message = client.messages.create(
    model="{{data.models.pfour_sonnet_id}}",
    max_tokens=1024,
    system="You're a support agent for the broadband provider Liffey Broadband…",
    messages=[{"role": "user", "content": "The internet's down AGAIN!!!"}],
)
```

Even a single sentence about the role changes the tone and focus of the reply.
:::

  - text: "The API key, so Claude knows who's calling it"
    correct: false
    why: "The key is sent in the x-api-key header and never goes into the prompt text. Claude doesn't need it."

:::

::: step explain
A strong system prompt has the same parts as project instructions:

- *role*: who to be;
- *context and why*: what the product is and why the rules are the way they are;
- *rules*: what to do in typical and tricky cases;
- *format*: the length and shape of the reply.

Write rules as “what to do” and explain the reason: Claude generalises from an explanation better than from a bare ban.
:::

::: exercise build_prompt
id: "p4.3.e2"
type: "build_prompt"
question: "Build a system prompt for the provider's bot. Leave out the blocks you don't need."
blocks:
  - id: "b1"
    text: "You're a support agent for the broadband provider Liffey Broadband."
    role: "role"
    why: "The role rules out lectures on how the internet works: Claude replies as a support agent, not an encyclopaedia."
  - id: "b2"
    text: "Customers write in when their internet has already stopped working, often from their phone and stressed, so they need a clear first step fast."
    role: "context"
    why: "The reason explains the rules: knowing the customer is annoyed and on their phone, Claude will choose short, specific replies on its own."
  - id: "b3"
    text: "First suggest checking the router: switch it off for 30 seconds and back on. If that doesn't help, ask for their account number and book an engineer."
    role: "goal"
    why: "A specific sequence of actions for the main scenario. Without it, every reply is improvised."
  - id: "b4"
    text: "Be polite and professional, 2–4 sentences, with any steps as a numbered list."
    role: "format"
    why: "The format is given as “what to do”: length, tone and how to show the steps."
  - id: "b5"
    text: "NEVER WRITE LONG REPLIES!!!"
    role: "noise"
    why: "All caps and an over-the-top ban. The format block already said how long the reply should be, calmly and specifically."
  - id: "b6"
    text: "API key: sk-ant-api03-…"
    role: "noise"
    why: "A key in the prompt is a leak. It's sent in the request header; Claude doesn't need to know it."
target_order: ["b1", "b2", "b3", "b4"]
hint: "Same order as project instructions: who you are, what the situation is and why, what to do, how to format the reply."
:::

::: exercise find_error
id: "p4.3.e3"
type: "find_error"
question: "Natalie is writing a system prompt for a booking bot at a dental practice in Cork. Tap the weak spots."
prompt: "You're the receptionist at White Fang Dental in Cork. Don't use markdown. Don't discuss prices. Only offer times from the schedule in the <schedule> tag, because booking without a free slot will disrupt another patient's appointment."
segments:
  - text: "You're the receptionist at White Fang Dental in Cork. "
    weak: false
    why: "A good role: it's clear who to be and where."
  - text: "Don't use markdown. "
    weak: true
    why: "A ban with no reason and no alternative. Better: “Write in plain text, with no asterisks or hashes: the reply is shown in a text message, where formatting doesn't work.”"
  - text: "Don't discuss prices. "
    weak: true
    why: "It's unclear what to do when someone asks about prices, and why. Better: “If asked about prices, say the dentist will give a quote after the check-up, and offer to book a consultation.”"
  - text: "Only offer times from the schedule in the <schedule> tag, because booking without a free slot will disrupt another patient's appointment."
    weak: false
    why: "Excellent: there's a rule, a data source in a tag and a reason. Claude will understand why it can't make up times."
hint: "Look for the “don't” rules: do they say what to do instead, and why?"
:::

::: step explain
When data goes into the system prompt (a schedule, a price list, documents), separate it with *XML tags*: `<schedule>…</schedule>`, `<faq>…</faq>`.

Tags help Claude tell instructions apart from data. Pick clear names and use them consistently throughout the prompt.
:::

::: step summary
**The system prompt is the app's standing briefing: role, context with the reason, rules and format.**

Data goes in XML tags, rules are written as “what to do”, and the key goes only in the request header.
